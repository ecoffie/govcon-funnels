### Two framing rules from that PRD — non-negotiable in any pitch

1. **Never "your set-aside isn't working."** Always *"you created the opportunity — here is how the
   competitive field could be expanded."* The acquisition decision and the market response are
   separate facts. We comment only on the second.
2. **Never say "capable."** The PRD's §2 correction: calling firms "capable" borrows regulatory
   weight we have not earned — we measured NAICS registration plus federal past performance. In
   front of SAME's A/E/C audience the first question is facility clearance and NAVSEA quality
   certification, and the honest answer may be single digits. Say **"relevant supplier pool"** or
   **"market-qualified."** This matters more at SAME than anywhere else.

**Why one offer isn't a supplier problem: using market intelligence to build competition before
solicitation.**

The specific case: a Navy shipyard set aside a $12.7M ship-repair requirement for small business —
the correct call — and received **one offer**. When we measured the relevant supplier pool for that

past performance, about 30 holding relevant certifications. Four of them appear anywhere in the
award record we sampled.

**market availability** and **market participation** — and whether market research can close that
gap *before* a solicitation posts rather than explaining it afterward.
