Lesson Content

1. Why Buyer Intelligence Matters

Buyer intelligence is the foundation of effective business development. Understanding who buys what, from whom, and for how much allows you to:

  • Target the right opportunities - Focus on agencies that actually buy what you sell
  • Position competitively - Know who you’re competing against
  • Time your outreach - Understand when contracts are expiring
  • Build relationships - Identify the right contacts

Your Role: Even if you’re not in BD, recognizing buyer patterns helps you support growth efforts and spot opportunities in your day-to-day work.

2. Introduction to SAM.gov Contract Data

SAM.gov Contract Data is now the official source for federal contract award information. (Note: This functionality was previously on FPDS.gov, which was retired in February 2026.)

What SAM.gov Contract Data Contains: - Contract award details (who won, how much, when) - Contracting agency and office - NAICS codes and PSC codes - Contract type (FFP, T&M, IDIQ, etc.) - Small business designations - Period of performance

What It Does NOT Contain: - Future opportunities (use SAM.gov Contract Opportunities for that) - Classified contracts - Grants or cooperative agreements

3. Navigating SAM.gov Contract Data

Accessing Contract Data: 1. Go to https://sam.gov 2. Create a free account (required to search contract data) 3. Click “Contract Data” in the main navigation 4. Select “Contract Award Search” to find awards

Key Search Fields:

Field What It Does Example
Vendor Name Find contracts awarded to a specific company “Booz Allen”
Contracting Agency Filter by agency “Department of Veterans Affairs”
NAICS Code Filter by industry classification “541512” (Computer Systems Design)
PSC Code Filter by product/service category “D302” (IT Systems Development)
Date Range Limit to specific fiscal years FY2024-FY2025
Award Amount Filter by contract size “$1M to $10M”

Tip: If you don’t have a SAM.gov account yet, USAspending.gov provides similar data without requiring an account, though SAM.gov offers more granular search capabilities.

4. Understanding NAICS and PSC Codes

NAICS Codes (North American Industry Classification System): - 6-digit codes classifying businesses by industry - Used for size standard determination - Example: 541512 = Computer Systems Design Services

PSC Codes (Product Service Codes): - 4-character codes describing what the government bought - More specific than NAICS - Example: D302 = IT and Telecom - Systems Development

Why Both Matter: - NAICS tells you what industry the contractor is in - PSC tells you what specific service/product was purchased - Use both to find relevant opportunities

5. Researching Agency Spending Patterns

Key Questions to Answer:

  1. How much does this agency spend?
    • Total contract dollars annually
    • Trend over past 3-5 years
  2. What do they buy?
    • Top PSC codes
    • Top NAICS codes
  3. Who do they buy from?
    • Top contractors by award value
    • Small business vs. large business split
  4. How do they buy?
    • Contract vehicles used (GSA, agency IDIQ, standalone)
    • Competition type (full & open, set-aside, sole source)
  5. When do contracts expire?
    • Recompete opportunities
    • Option year timing

6. Incumbent Research

Why Research Incumbents: - Understand current performance expectations - Identify potential teaming partners - Assess competitive landscape - Find recompete opportunities

What to Look For:

Data Point Where to Find Why It Matters
Contract value SAM.gov Contract Data Size of opportunity
Period of performance SAM.gov Contract Data When it expires
Contract type SAM.gov Contract Data Risk profile
Past modifications SAM.gov Contract Data Scope changes, issues
Other agency work SAM.gov Contract Data Incumbent’s breadth
Company size DSBS, SAM.gov Teaming considerations

7. Using USAspending for Agency Analysis

USAspending.gov provides a more user-friendly view of spending data.

Key Features:

  1. Agency Profiles: Overview of each agency’s spending
  2. Recipient Profiles: See all awards to a specific contractor
  3. Spending Explorer: Drill down by category, location, time
  4. Award Search: Find specific contracts

When to Use Each Tool:

Task Best Tool
Agency overview USAspending
Specific contract details SAM.gov Contract Data
Contractor research Both
Historical trends USAspending
Detailed award data SAM.gov Contract Data

AI Accelerator: Using AI for Buyer Intelligence

AI tools can help you analyze SAM.gov contract data, research incumbents, and synthesize buyer intelligence faster. Use these prompts to accelerate your research.

Prompt 1: Analyze Contract Award Data

Use When: You’ve exported SAM.gov contract data and need help making sense of it.

I pulled the following contract award data from SAM.gov for [AGENCY NAME]:

[PASTE YOUR CONTRACT DATA - contractor names, award amounts, dates, NAICS codes]

Please analyze this data and tell me:
1. Who are the dominant contractors and what's their market share?
2. What patterns do you see in contract types (FFP vs T&M vs CPFF)?
3. Are there any contractors that appear to be growing or declining?
4. What's the average contract size and duration?
5. Any insights for a company trying to enter this market?

Prompt 2: Incumbent Contractor Profile

Use When: You need to research a specific competitor or incumbent.

I'm researching [COMPETITOR NAME], a federal contractor. Based on general
knowledge about government contracting, help me build an intel profile:

1. What should I look for in SAM.gov Contract Data to understand their contract portfolio?
2. What are typical strengths of established incumbents?
3. What vulnerabilities might a long-term incumbent have?
4. How can I find out about their subcontracting relationships?
5. What questions should I be asking about this competitor?

Note: I'll verify all specific data against official sources.

Prompt 3: NAICS/PSC Code Research

Use When: You need to understand which codes are relevant to your services.

Our company provides [DESCRIBE YOUR SERVICES - e.g., "IT helpdesk support
and cybersecurity services to federal agencies"].

Help me identify:
1. The most relevant NAICS codes for our services
2. The most relevant PSC codes we should be tracking
3. Which codes typically have small business set-asides
4. Any related codes we might be missing
5. How to search SAM.gov Contract Data using these codes effectively

Prompt 4: Summarize Agency Buyer Profile

Use When: You need to create a briefing on an agency’s buying patterns.

I've researched [AGENCY NAME] and found the following data:

- Total spending: [AMOUNT]
- Top contractors: [LIST]
- Top PSC codes: [LIST]

Please help me create a 1-page executive briefing that:
1. Summarizes the agency's procurement profile
2. Identifies opportunities for a company offering [YOUR SERVICES]
3. Highlights potential challenges or barriers to entry
4. Recommends 3 specific next steps for pursuing this agency

Prompt 5: Contract Recompete Analysis

Use When: You’ve identified a contract that’s expiring and want to analyze the opportunity.

I found a contract in SAM.gov Contract Data that's expiring soon:

- Contract Number: [NUMBER]
- Incumbent: [COMPANY]
- Original Value: [AMOUNT]
- Period of Performance: [DATES]
- Contract Type: [TYPE]
- NAICS: [CODE]
- Agency: [NAME]

Help me think through:
1. What does this contract scope likely include?
2. What questions should I research before pursuing?
3. What might the incumbent's advantages be?
4. How should we position against the incumbent?
5. What's a realistic timeline for engaging on the recompete?

Pro Tips for AI-Assisted Buyer Intelligence

Do Don’t
Export SAM.gov contract data and paste into AI for analysis Ask AI for current contract values (verify in SAM.gov)
Use AI to identify patterns in large datasets Rely on AI for competitor revenue figures
Ask AI to help structure your research approach Skip verification against official sources
Use AI to draft briefings from your research Present AI analysis as verified intelligence

Remember: AI helps you work faster, but SAM.gov Contract Data and USAspending are your authoritative sources. Always verify before sharing intelligence with your team.

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded—use them to confirm your understanding.

Question 1 (Application)

You want to find all IT services contracts awarded by the VA in FY2025. Which SAM.gov Contract Data search fields would you use?

  1. Vendor Name and Award Amount
  2. Contracting Agency, NAICS Code, and Date Range
  3. PSC Code only
  4. Contract Type and Place of Performance

Answer: B - You’d filter by Contracting Agency (VA), NAICS Code (541512 or similar IT code), and Date Range (FY2025).

Question 2 (Recall)

What is the difference between NAICS codes and PSC codes?

  1. NAICS codes are for products, PSC codes are for services
  2. NAICS codes classify the contractor’s industry, PSC codes describe what was purchased
  3. NAICS codes are used by DoD only, PSC codes are government-wide
  4. There is no difference; they are interchangeable

Answer: B - NAICS classifies the business/industry; PSC describes the specific product or service purchased.

Question 3 (Scenario)

Your company wants to pursue a VA contract currently held by a competitor. Using SAM.gov Contract Data, you discover the contract expires in 6 months with no remaining options. What does this tell you?

  1. The contract will automatically renew
  2. A recompete opportunity is likely coming soon
  3. The incumbent will definitely win again
  4. The VA no longer needs this service

Answer: B - An expiring contract with no options typically means the agency will need to recompete or issue a new solicitation.

Question 4 (Application)

You’re researching agency spending trends. Which tool provides the easiest view of year-over-year spending changes?

  1. SAM.gov Contract Data
  2. SAM.gov
  3. USAspending.gov Agency Profiles
  4. DSBS

Answer: C - USAspending Agency Profiles provide visual spending trends over time.

Question 5 (Recall)

What is the minimum contract value typically reported in SAM.gov Contract Data?

  1. $1,000
  2. $10,000
  3. $25,000
  4. $100,000

Answer: B - Contract actions over $10,000 are reported in SAM.gov Contract Data (with some exceptions).

Question 6 (Scenario)

You find that an agency’s top contractor holds 40% of all contract dollars, with the next largest at 8%. What does this suggest?

  1. The agency has a healthy competitive environment
  2. There may be a dominant incumbent with strong relationships
  3. Small businesses are well represented
  4. The agency prefers sole-source contracts

Answer: B - A single contractor holding 40% suggests concentration, which could indicate strong incumbent advantage or limited competition.
