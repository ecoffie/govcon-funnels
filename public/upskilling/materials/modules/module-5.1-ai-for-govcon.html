Why AI Matters for GovCon

The Efficiency Multiplier

AI tools can dramatically accelerate GovCon BD work:

Task Without AI With AI Time Savings
Summarize 50-page RFP 2-3 hours 15-30 minutes 80%+
Research agency spending 1-2 hours 20-30 minutes 70%+
Draft past performance narrative 2-3 hours 30-45 minutes 75%+
Analyze competitor portfolio 1-2 hours 15-30 minutes 75%+
Create compliance matrix (initial) 2-4 hours 30-60 minutes 70%+

The Reality Check

AI is powerful but has limits:

AI Does Well AI Does Poorly
Summarizing large documents Accessing real-time data
Drafting content for human review Making strategic decisions
Analyzing patterns in data you provide Verifying facts independently
Explaining complex topics Knowing your specific company
Brainstorming options Understanding customer relationships
Structuring information Replacing human judgment

The Rule: AI is your research assistant, not your decision-maker. Always verify, always review, always apply human judgment.

2. The Anatomy of a Good Prompt

Effective prompts have consistent structure:

The CRAFT Framework:

Element Description Example
Context Background information “I’m a BD analyst at a small IT services company…”
Role Who AI should act as “Act as an experienced capture manager…”
Action What you want done “Analyze this RFP and identify all requirements…”
Format How to structure output “Provide as a numbered list with…”
Tone Style of response “Use professional, concise language…”

Example Prompt Using CRAFT:

Context: I'm preparing for a capture kickoff meeting for a VA IT
modernization opportunity worth $15M.

Role: Act as an experienced capture manager who has won similar
VA contracts.

Action: Help me create a capture kickoff agenda that identifies
the key questions we need to answer, initial win themes to test,
and first 30-day milestones.

Format: Provide as a structured agenda with timing for a 2-hour
meeting, followed by a bulleted action item list.

Tone: Professional and direct, suitable for a senior audience.

3. Prompt Engineering Best Practices

Do This:

Practice Why It Works
Be specific “VA IT services” beats “government work”
Provide examples “Format like this example…”
Set constraints “In 200 words or less…”
Ask for structure “Provide as a table with columns for…”
Request reasoning “Explain your analysis…”
Iterate “Now refine this to emphasize…”

Avoid This:

Mistake Better Approach
Vague requests Add specifics about what you need
Single long prompt Break into steps, iterate
Accepting first output Ask for refinements
No context Explain your situation
Asking for opinions Ask for analysis with criteria

4. AI for Each BD Phase

Market Research & Intelligence

PROMPT: Agency Research Summary

I need to research [AGENCY NAME] as a potential target for our
[SERVICE TYPE] offerings.

Using general knowledge about federal agencies, provide:
1. Agency mission overview (2-3 sentences)
2. Major components/bureaus
3. Typical procurement priorities for agencies like this
4. Key initiatives or modernization efforts to research
5. Suggested data to pull from USAspending and SAM.gov Contract Data
6. Questions I should answer about this agency

Note: I'll verify all specific data against official sources.
PROMPT: Contract Award Data Analysis

I pulled this contract data from SAM.gov Contract Data for [AGENCY/NAICS]:

[PASTE YOUR EXPORTED DATA]

Analyze and provide:
1. Top contractors by award value
2. Patterns in contract types (FFP vs T&M)
3. Average contract size and duration
4. Trends over time (growing/shrinking?)
5. Implications for a newcomer trying to enter this market

Opportunity Discovery & Qualification

PROMPT: Opportunity Summary

Summarize this SAM.gov opportunity for a quick team review:

[PASTE FULL OPPORTUNITY TEXT]

Provide:
1. Executive summary (3 sentences)
2. Key requirements (bullets)
3. Deadlines and timeline
4. Set-aside and eligibility
5. Initial assessment: Is this worth pursuing for a company
   that provides [YOUR SERVICES]?
PROMPT: Go/No-Go Assessment

Evaluate this opportunity against go/no-go criteria:

Opportunity:
[PASTE KEY DETAILS]

Our Company:
- Capabilities: [LIST]
- Certifications: [LIST]
- Relevant past performance: [YES/NO/LIMITED]
- Customer relationship: [STRONG/SOME/NONE]
- Available resources: [HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW]

Score each factor (Green/Yellow/Red) and provide overall
recommendation with key risks.

Capture & Competitive Positioning

PROMPT: Win Theme Development

Develop win themes for this opportunity:

Opportunity: [DESCRIPTION]
Customer Pain Points: [IF KNOWN]
Our Strengths: [LIST]
Competitor Likely Strengths: [LIST]

Create 3-5 win themes using format:
"Because of [capability], we deliver [benefit] that
[addresses priority], proven by [evidence]."

Make them specific, provable, and differentiating.
PROMPT: Competitive Ghosting

Our main competitor is the incumbent with these characteristics:
- [CHARACTERISTIC 1]
- [CHARACTERISTIC 2]
- [POTENTIAL WEAKNESS]

Our corresponding strengths:
- [STRENGTH 1]
- [STRENGTH 2]

Write 3-5 ghosting statements that highlight our strengths
without naming the competitor. These should be suitable
for proposal narrative.

Proposal Support

PROMPT: RFP Requirements Extraction

Extract all requirements from this RFP section:

[PASTE SECTION L OR C]

Format as a compliance matrix with:
- RFP Reference
- Requirement Description
- Type (Shall/Should/May)
- Notes on compliance approach

Flag any ambiguous requirements or questions to ask.
PROMPT: Past Performance Narrative

Write a past performance narrative:

Contract: [NAME]
Customer: [AGENCY]
Value: [AMOUNT]
Period: [DATES]
Work Performed: [DESCRIPTION]
Results: [OUTCOMES]
Relevance: [HOW IT'S SIMILAR TO CURRENT OPP]

Write in [X] words, emphasizing relevance and results.
Use active voice and quantify outcomes where possible.
PROMPT: Proposal Section Draft

Draft a proposal section responding to this requirement:

Requirement: [PASTE RFP REQUIREMENT]

Our Approach: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
Key Proof Points: [RELEVANT EXPERIENCE]
Win Theme: [THEME TO EMPHASIZE]

Write [X] words that:
- Addresses all requirement elements
- Leads with our strength
- Includes specific proof points
- Uses "will" not "would"

5. The AI Workflow for GovCon

Step-by-Step Process:

1. GATHER: Collect raw information (RFP, SAM.gov contract data, opportunity)
   ↓
2. PROMPT: Ask AI to analyze, summarize, or draft
   ↓
3. REVIEW: Read AI output critically
   ↓
4. VERIFY: Check facts against authoritative sources
   ↓
5. REFINE: Ask AI to improve based on your feedback
   ↓
6. CUSTOMIZE: Add your knowledge, relationships, strategy
   ↓
7. FINALIZE: Human makes final decisions and edits

Key Checkpoints:

Stage Human Must Do
After PROMPT Check if output is on-target
After REVIEW Identify gaps, errors, or weak areas
After VERIFY Confirm all facts against sources
After REFINE Ensure it reflects your actual strategy
FINALIZE Add expert judgment, relationship context

6. AI Ethics and Security

What NOT to Share with AI:

Never Share Why
Proprietary pricing Competitive sensitivity
Source selection info Procurement integrity
Customer PII Privacy regulations
Classified information Security requirements
Trade secrets Competitive protection
Internal win strategies Competitive sensitivity

Safe Practices:

  • Use general terms when possible (“a federal agency” vs. specific names)
  • Sanitize data before pasting (remove PII, sensitive details)
  • Use enterprise versions of AI tools when available
  • Follow your company’s AI usage policy
  • When in doubt, don’t share it

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Question 1 (Application)

You need to summarize a 75-page RFP for your team. What’s the best AI approach?

  1. Paste the entire document and ask for a summary
  2. Break into sections, summarize each, then synthesize
  3. Ask AI to find the RFP online and summarize it
  4. AI can’t handle documents that long

Answer: B - Breaking into sections produces better results and stays within token limits. Always provide the content to AI rather than asking it to find documents.

Question 2 (Recall)

What does the “C” in the CRAFT prompt framework stand for?

  1. Clarity
  2. Context
  3. Command
  4. Constraint

Answer: B - Context provides background information that helps AI understand your situation and produce relevant outputs.

Question 3 (Application)

You’re using AI to draft a past performance narrative. What should you do after AI generates the draft?

  1. Submit it directly to the proposal
  2. Verify facts, add your expertise, and refine
  3. Ask AI to verify its own work
  4. Nothing—AI output is ready to use

Answer: B - AI drafts require human review, fact verification, and refinement with your specific knowledge before use.

Question 4 (Ethics)

Which of the following is safe to share with AI tools?

  1. Your company’s proprietary pricing strategy
  2. A sanitized summary of an agency’s public spending data
  3. Source selection information from a customer
  4. Customer personal information from your CRM

Answer: B - Public data that you’ve gathered from official sources is safe. Never share proprietary, source selection, or personal information.

Question 5 (Application)

Your AI-generated win theme says “We have extensive federal experience.” How should you improve it?

  1. It’s fine—clear and professional
  2. Ask AI to add specific proof points and quantify
  3. Remove it—AI shouldn’t write win themes
  4. Add more adjectives to make it stronger

Answer: B - Generic claims need specifics. Ask AI to add proof points: “Our team has delivered 12 similar projects to VA over 8 years with 100% on-time delivery.”

Question 6 (Recall)

What’s the most important step after getting AI output?

  1. Ask AI to improve it
  2. Share it with your team
  3. Verify against authoritative sources
  4. Format it nicely

Answer: C - Verification is critical. AI can be confidently wrong. Always check facts against official sources like SAM.gov Contract Data, USAspending, and agency websites.
