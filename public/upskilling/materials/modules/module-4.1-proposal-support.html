Lesson Content

1. The Proposal Development Process

Proposal development is a structured process that transforms capture intelligence and solution designs into a compliant, compelling response.

Typical Proposal Timeline (30-day example):

Phase Days Activities
Pre-Proposal Before RFP Capture activities, solution development, draft content
RFP Analysis 1-3 Compliance matrix, kickoff, assignments
Writing 4-15 Draft content, graphics, data calls
Pink Team Review 16-18 First major review (outline/early draft)
Revisions 19-22 Incorporate feedback, refine
Red Team Review 23-25 Near-final review
Final Revisions 26-28 Final polishing
Production 29-30 Format, page check, submit

Key Principle: The better the capture work, the easier the proposal. Most “proposal problems” are really “capture problems.”

2. Proposal Team Roles

Role Responsibilities
Capture Manager Leads pre-proposal; transitions to proposal; owns win strategy
Proposal Manager Runs the proposal process; schedule, compliance, production
Volume Leads Own specific volumes (technical, management, past performance)
Writers/Contributors Draft proposal content
Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) Provide technical accuracy and detail
Reviewers Evaluate drafts against RFP and win strategy
Pricing Lead Develops cost/price volume
Contracts Reviews terms, reps/certs, ensures compliance
Graphics Creates visuals, charts, diagrams
Production Final formatting, assembly, submission

Your Role: Even if you’re not a dedicated proposal professional, you may be asked to: - Provide technical input or data - Review sections for accuracy - Gather past performance information - Support data calls (staffing, resumes, etc.) - Help with final production

3. Understanding RFP Structure

A typical RFP contains several key sections:

Section What It Contains Why It Matters
Section A Solicitation form (SF 33 or SF 1449) Basic contract info
Section B Supplies/Services and Prices What you’re bidding on
Section C Statement of Work (SOW) or PWS Technical requirements
Section L Instructions to Offerors How to prepare your proposal
Section M Evaluation Factors How you’ll be scored
Attachments CLINs, data requirements, templates Supporting documents

Critical Sections for Proposal Compliance:

  • Section L: Tells you exactly what to submit, format requirements, page limits
  • Section M: Tells you how you’ll be evaluated and what matters most

Example Section L Instructions: > “The Technical Volume shall not exceed 50 pages. Use 12-point Times New Roman font with 1-inch margins. Include a staffing plan, technical approach, and transition plan…”

Example Section M Evaluation Factors: > “Technical Approach (40%), Past Performance (30%), Price (30%). Technical and Past Performance are approximately equal and significantly more important than price.”

4. Building a Compliance Matrix

The compliance matrix is the backbone of proposal development. It maps every RFP requirement to your proposal response.

Why It Matters: - Ensures nothing is missed - Assigns responsibility for each requirement - Tracks compliance status - Guides evaluators to your responses

Basic Compliance Matrix Structure:

RFP Ref Requirement Proposal Section Page Compliant? Owner
L.4.1 Provide staffing plan Vol 1, Sec 3.1 12-15 ✓ J. Smith
L.4.2 Include org chart Vol 1, Sec 3.2 16 ✓ J. Smith
C.3.1 24x7 support capability Vol 1, Sec 2.3 8-9 ✓ T. Jones
M.1 Technical Approach Vol 1, Sec 2 5-20 ✓ Lead

Building a Compliance Matrix Step-by-Step:

  1. Read Section L completely - Extract every instruction
  2. Read Section M completely - Note all evaluation factors
  3. Read Section C (SOW/PWS) - Identify all requirements
  4. Create requirement rows - One row per requirement
  5. Map to proposal sections - Where will you address each?
  6. Assign owners - Who’s responsible?
  7. Track completion - Update as proposal develops

5. Common Proposal Volumes

Most proposals have separate volumes for different content:

Volume Content Typical Focus
Volume I: Technical Technical approach, solution, staffing, management How you’ll do the work
Volume II: Past Performance Relevant experience, references Proof you can do it
Volume III: Price/Cost Pricing, cost breakdown What it will cost
Volume IV: Administrative Reps/certs, subcontracting plan Compliance documents

Technical Volume Key Sections: - Technical Approach: How you’ll accomplish the work - Management Approach: How you’ll manage the contract - Staffing Plan: Who will do the work - Transition Plan: How you’ll start up smoothly - Quality Control: How you’ll ensure quality

Past Performance Key Elements: - Contract name and number - Customer contact information - Relevance to current requirement - Contract value and period - Description of work performed - Performance outcomes/metrics

6. Evaluation Factors and What Evaluators Look For

Understanding evaluation factors helps you prioritize your proposal content.

Typical Evaluation Factor Structure:

Factor Weight What Evaluators Assess
Technical/Management 40-50% Solution quality, understanding, approach
Past Performance 25-35% Relevant experience, performance quality
Price 15-30% Cost realism, competitiveness
Small Business 5-15% Subcontracting plan (large business only)

Evaluation Ratings (Common Scale):

Rating Definition
Outstanding Exceptional; significantly exceeds requirements
Good Meets and occasionally exceeds requirements
Acceptable Meets minimum requirements
Marginal Fails to meet some requirements
Unacceptable Fails to meet requirements; not awardable

What Makes a Proposal “Outstanding”: - Clear compliance with all requirements - Specific, relevant past performance - Concrete proof points, not generalities - Thoughtful understanding of customer’s challenges - Innovative approaches that add value - Strong, relevant team

7. Supporting Proposals: Common Tasks

When asked to support a proposal, you may be assigned to:

Data Calls: Provide specific information - Resume updates - Past performance narratives - Technical specifications - Pricing inputs - Certifications/licenses

Content Review: Check sections for accuracy - Technical accuracy - Company information correctness - Consistency with company capabilities - Grammar and clarity

Research Support: - Incumbent research (SAM.gov Contract Data) - Customer intel gathering - Competitive analysis support - Regulation/policy research

Production Support: - Formatting documents - Checking page counts - Assembling final package - Quality control checks

8. Proposal Dos and Don’ts

Do: - Read the RFP carefully before starting - Follow all instructions exactly (page limits, fonts, format) - Be specific with examples and proof points - Address every requirement explicitly - Use clear, direct language - Highlight your discriminators and win themes - Meet all deadlines (internal and external)

Don’t: - Submit generic boilerplate content - Miss page limits or format requirements - Make claims you can’t prove - Criticize competitors by name - Assume evaluators know your company - Wait until the last minute - Ignore evaluation criteria weighting

AI Accelerator: Using AI for Proposal Support

AI tools can dramatically accelerate proposal development—from RFP analysis to content drafting. Use these prompts to support proposal efforts effectively.

Prompt 1: Analyze RFP Requirements

Use When: You need to quickly understand what an RFP requires.

Analyze this RFP section and extract all requirements:

[PASTE SECTION L OR SECTION C TEXT]

Provide:
1. Explicit requirements (stated directly)
2. Implicit requirements (implied but not stated)
3. Compliance items (specific deliverables or formats required)
4. Potential ambiguities or questions to ask
5. Any unusual or non-standard requirements

Format as a requirements checklist I can use for compliance tracking.

Prompt 2: Summarize Evaluation Criteria

Use When: You need to understand how the proposal will be scored.

Analyze this Section M (Evaluation Factors):

[PASTE SECTION M TEXT]

Help me understand:
1. What are the evaluation factors and their weights?
2. What does "Outstanding" look like for each factor?
3. What are the discriminating factors (where we can win or lose)?
4. What's the relationship between price and technical?
5. What should we emphasize most in our response?

Create a scoring guide for our proposal team.

Prompt 3: Build Initial Compliance Matrix

Use When: You need to start a compliance matrix quickly.

Create a compliance matrix from this RFP section:

[PASTE SECTION L INSTRUCTIONS]

Format as a table with columns:
- RFP Reference (section number)
- Requirement Description
- Proposed Response Location
- Compliance Status (TBD)
- Assigned Owner (TBD)
- Notes

Include every requirement, including formatting and page limits.

Prompt 4: Draft Proposal Section Outline

Use When: You need to structure a proposal section before writing.

Help me outline a proposal section for:

RFP Requirement: [PASTE SPECIFIC REQUIREMENT]
Evaluation Factor: [WHICH FACTOR THIS ADDRESSES]
Page Limit: [IF APPLICABLE]

Our approach: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF OUR SOLUTION]
Key proof points: [RELEVANT EXPERIENCE]
Win theme to emphasize: [THEME]

Create an outline that:
1. Addresses all requirements explicitly
2. Leads with our strength/discriminator
3. Includes proof points and evidence
4. Follows a logical flow
5. Can be expanded into full narrative

Prompt 5: Draft Past Performance Narrative

Use When: You need to write a past performance citation.

Help me draft a past performance narrative:

Contract Information:
- Contract Name: [NAME]
- Customer: [AGENCY]
- Contract Number: [NUMBER]
- Value: [AMOUNT]
- Period: [DATES]
- Your Role: [PRIME/SUB]

Work Performed:
- [DESCRIBE THE WORK]

Relevance to Current Opportunity:
- Similar scope: [HOW]
- Similar size: [HOW]
- Similar complexity: [HOW]

Results/Outcomes:
- [QUANTIFIED ACHIEVEMENTS]

Draft a past performance narrative that:
1. Clearly shows relevance to the current RFP
2. Emphasizes successful outcomes with metrics
3. Demonstrates our capabilities
4. Fits in [X] words/page limit

Prompt 6: Review Draft for Compliance

Use When: You want to check if a draft section meets requirements.

Review this draft proposal section against the RFP requirement:

RFP Requirement:
[PASTE THE REQUIREMENT]

Our Draft:
[PASTE YOUR DRAFT CONTENT]

Check for:
1. Does it address all parts of the requirement?
2. Are there any compliance gaps?
3. Is the response clear and direct?
4. Are claims supported with evidence?
5. Does it emphasize our strengths?
6. What's missing or could be improved?

Provide specific recommendations for strengthening this section.

Prompt 7: Improve Proposal Language

Use When: You have draft content that needs strengthening.

Improve this proposal content to be more compelling:

Original:
[PASTE YOUR DRAFT TEXT]

Requirements:
- Make it more specific (add details)
- Strengthen weak claims with proof
- Remove vague language ("various," "many," "significant")
- Add action verbs and active voice
- Emphasize benefits to the customer
- Keep the same approximate length

Provide the improved version and explain key changes made.

Pro Tips for AI-Assisted Proposal Support

Do Don’t
Use AI to extract requirements from RFPs Submit AI-generated content without review
Get AI help outlining proposal sections Let AI access actual proposal files
Draft past performance narratives with AI Use AI for pricing or cost volumes
Check compliance with AI assistance Share proprietary win strategies with AI
Improve language and clarity with AI Skip human review of AI-generated content

Remember: AI accelerates proposal work but doesn’t replace expertise. Always have subject matter experts review AI-assisted content, and never submit AI output without thorough human review.

AI Proposal Workflow

  1. RFP Analysis: Use AI to extract requirements → Human verifies
  2. Compliance Matrix: AI builds initial matrix → Human validates
  3. Outline: AI creates structure → Human refines
  4. Draft: AI generates first draft → Human rewrites substantially
  5. Improve: AI enhances language → Human reviews and approves
  6. Review: AI checks compliance → Human makes final call

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded.

Question 1 (Recall)

What section of an RFP contains the instructions for how to prepare your proposal?

  1. Section A
  2. Section C
  3. Section L
  4. Section M

Answer: C - Section L contains “Instructions to Offerors” that specify format, page limits, and submission requirements.

Question 2 (Application)

The RFP states: “Technical Volume shall not exceed 50 pages using 12-point font.” Your draft is 55 pages. What should you do?

  1. Submit it anyway—evaluators will understand
  2. Use a smaller font to fit more content
  3. Edit content to meet the 50-page limit exactly
  4. Add an appendix for the extra pages

Answer: C - Page limits are strict compliance requirements. Exceeding them can result in pages being ignored or your proposal being rejected.

Question 3 (Recall)

What is the purpose of a compliance matrix?

  1. To track project milestones after award
  2. To map every RFP requirement to your proposal response
  3. To calculate your price
  4. To evaluate competitors

Answer: B - The compliance matrix ensures every RFP requirement is addressed and shows where each response appears in your proposal.

Question 4 (Scenario)

You’re supporting a proposal and asked to provide a past performance narrative. What information should you include?

  1. Just the contract number
  2. Contract name, value, customer contact, relevance, work description, and outcomes
  3. A general description of your company
  4. Competitor comparison

Answer: B - Past performance narratives need specific details: contract information, customer contact, relevance to current opportunity, description of work, and measurable outcomes.

Question 5 (Application)

The evaluation factors state: “Technical (40%), Past Performance (30%), Price (30%). Technical and Past Performance are significantly more important than Price.” What does this tell you?

  1. Price is the most important factor
  2. Focus your strongest effort on Technical and Past Performance
  3. All factors are weighted equally
  4. You should submit the lowest price possible

Answer: B - When non-price factors outweigh price, the government may pay more for a superior technical/past performance proposal. Focus on strength there.

Question 6 (Recall)

What is a “Pink Team” review?

  1. Final review before submission
  2. Early review of proposal outline or initial draft
  3. Customer review of your proposal
  4. Competitor analysis review

Answer: B - Pink Team is typically the first major review, focused on outline completeness, compliance, and early draft content. Red Team is the near-final review.
