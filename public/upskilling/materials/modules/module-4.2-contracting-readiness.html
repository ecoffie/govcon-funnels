Lesson Content

1. Why Contracting Readiness Matters

Before you can win federal contracts, your company must be “ready” to do business with the government. This means:

  • Registration: Active in required systems
  • Certifications: Verified small business status (if applicable)
  • Compliance: Meeting FAR requirements
  • Infrastructure: Systems and processes in place

Key Insight: Lack of readiness can disqualify you from opportunities or delay awards. Being ready before opportunities arise is critical.

2. Essential Contractor Registrations

SAM.gov Registration

What: System for Award Management - the official government database for contractor registration.

Why Required: You cannot receive a federal contract without an active SAM registration.

Key Elements:

Element Description
UEI Unique Entity Identifier - replaced DUNS number
CAGE Code Commercial and Government Entity code
NAICS Codes Industry codes you operate under
Size Status Small business determination by NAICS
Reps & Certs Representations and certifications
Core Data Company information, POCs, banking

Registration Requirements: - Must be renewed annually - Must be active at time of award - Updates required for any changes - Free to register

Status Check: Go to SAM.gov → Entity Search → Search your company

Other Key Registrations

System Purpose Who Needs It
SAM.gov Entity registration All contractors
DSBS Small business profile Small businesses
WAWF Invoice submission All contractors
eSRS Subcontracting reports Large businesses
CPARS Past performance database All contractors (receiving ratings)
Agency-specific Varies Depends on agency

3. Small Business Certifications

Small business certifications provide competitive advantages through set-aside contracts.

SBA Certification Programs

Certification Who Qualifies Advantage
8(a) Socially and economically disadvantaged Sole source up to $4.5M (services), set-asides
HUBZone Located in Historically Underutilized Business Zone Set-asides, price evaluation preference
WOSB Women-owned (51%+) Set-asides in underrepresented industries
EDWOSB Economically disadvantaged women-owned Set-asides in all industries
SDVOSB Service-disabled veteran-owned (51%+) Set-asides, VA priority
VOSB Veteran-owned (51%+) VA set-asides

Self-Certification vs. SBA Certification

Type Examples Verification
Self-Certified WOSB (some), small business status Declare in SAM.gov; subject to protest
SBA-Certified 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB, EDWOSB SBA reviews and certifies; stronger protection

Note: Self-certification is easier but more vulnerable to challenges. SBA certification provides stronger standing.

4. FAR Basics for BD Professionals

The Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) governs all federal procurement. You don’t need to be a FAR expert, but understanding key concepts helps.

Key FAR Concepts

FAR Part Topic Relevance to BD
Part 2 Definitions Common terminology
Part 9 Contractor Qualifications Responsibility, debarment
Part 15 Contracting by Negotiation Proposal process, evaluation
Part 16 Contract Types FFP, T&M, CPFF, IDIQ
Part 19 Small Business Programs Set-asides, certifications

Common Contract Types

Type Full Name Risk Profile Use Case
FFP Firm Fixed Price Contractor bears cost risk Well-defined requirements
T&M Time and Materials Government bears cost risk Uncertain scope
CPFF Cost Plus Fixed Fee Shared risk R&D, complex work
IDIQ Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity Varies by task order Ongoing needs, flexibility
BPA Blanket Purchase Agreement Varies Recurring purchases

Compliance Requirements

Requirement Description How to Comply
Organizational Conflict of Interest (OCI) Avoid conflicts that bias recommendations Disclose potential conflicts; mitigate
Cost Accounting Standards (CAS) Consistent cost tracking For large contracts; proper accounting
Procurement Integrity No improper access to competitor info Follow FAR 3.104
Equal Employment Opportunity Non-discrimination EEO certifications in SAM.gov
Truthful Cost or Pricing Data Accurate pricing submissions For large negotiated contracts

5. Internal Handoffs: BD to Delivery

Winning a contract is only half the battle. Smooth transition from BD/capture to program execution is critical.

The Handoff Challenge

BD/Capture Knows Delivery Needs to Know
Customer pain points How to address them operationally
Win themes promised How to deliver on those promises
Key personnel committed Staffing plan execution
Pricing assumptions Actual cost management
Competitor weaknesses exploited Performance standards to meet

Key Handoff Documents

Document Purpose
Transition Plan How to start up without disruption
Staffing Plan Who, when, and how they’ll be onboarded
Risk Register Known risks from capture phase
Key Customer Contacts Relationships to transfer
Pricing Basis Assumptions behind the numbers
Technical Solution What was proposed
Compliance Matrix Requirements we committed to

Handoff Meeting Agenda (Example)

  1. Contract overview and key dates
  2. Customer expectations and pain points
  3. Win themes and promises made
  4. Key personnel and their roles
  5. Pricing assumptions and constraints
  6. Known risks and issues
  7. Critical first 30/60/90 day actions
  8. Questions and clarifications

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded.

Question 1 (Recall)

What is required for a company to receive a federal contract award?

  1. Only a capability statement
  2. Active SAM.gov registration
  3. Past performance only
  4. GSA Schedule

Answer: B - An active SAM.gov registration is required before any federal contract can be awarded.

Question 2 (Application)

Your company’s SAM.gov registration expires next month. What happens if it lapses?

  1. Nothing—it auto-renews
  2. You cannot receive new contract awards until renewed
  3. Current contracts are automatically cancelled
  4. You get a 90-day grace period

Answer: B - Without an active SAM registration, you cannot receive new contract awards. Existing contracts continue, but new awards cannot be made.

Question 3 (Recall)

Which small business certification allows sole-source contracts up to $4.5M for services?

  1. HUBZone
  2. WOSB
  3. 8(a)
  4. SDVOSB

Answer: C - The 8(a) program allows sole-source contracts up to $4.5M for services ($7M for manufacturing).

Question 4 (Scenario)

Your company wins a contract after making several commitments in the proposal. The program manager taking over wasn’t involved in capture. What’s the biggest risk?

  1. The program manager doesn’t know the customer’s name
  2. Promises made in the proposal aren’t delivered, damaging customer relationship
  3. The pricing is too high
  4. There’s no risk

Answer: B - Poor handoff from capture to delivery means the team may not know what was promised, leading to unmet expectations and damaged relationships.

Question 5 (Application)

Your BD team has a 10% win rate and leadership is concerned. What should you investigate first?

  1. Just submit more proposals
  2. Analyze loss reasons to identify patterns
  3. Lower all prices by 20%
  4. Stop pursuing opportunities

Answer: B - A low win rate signals a systemic issue. Win/loss analysis reveals whether it’s qualification, positioning, pricing, or proposal quality.

Question 6 (Recall)

What is the recommended pipeline value relative to annual revenue for a healthy BD operation?

  1. Equal to annual revenue
  2. 3-5x annual revenue
  3. 10x annual revenue
  4. Pipeline size doesn’t matter

Answer: B - A healthy pipeline is typically 3-5x annual revenue to account for win rates and timing of awards.
