Lesson Content

1. The BD Lifecycle: Big Picture

Business Development in GovCon is a structured process that transforms market intelligence into contract awards. Understanding this lifecycle helps everyone in the company contribute effectively.

The Three Phases:

Phase Focus Typical Duration Key Activities
BD Phase Finding & qualifying opportunities Ongoing/Long-term Market research, pipeline building, customer engagement
Capture Phase Positioning to win a specific opportunity 6-18 months before RFP Solution development, teaming, customer shaping
Proposal Phase Responding to solicitation 30-90 days (typical) Writing, compliance, pricing, submission

Key Insight: Most companies that win do the work in BD and Capture—long before the proposal phase begins.

2. BD Phase: Finding and Qualifying Opportunities

The BD phase is about building a healthy pipeline of qualified opportunities.

Core BD Activities:

Activity Description Output
Market Research Understanding agency spending, trends, competitors Market assessments
Opportunity Identification Finding relevant solicitations and forecasts Opportunity log
Qualification Deciding which opportunities to pursue Bid/no-bid decisions
Customer Engagement Building relationships with buyers Meeting notes, contacts
Pipeline Management Tracking opportunities through stages Pipeline reports

Pipeline Stages (Typical):

Stage Description Activities
Identified Opportunity found via SAM.gov, forecast, or intel Initial research
Qualified Passes initial go/no-go criteria Opportunity assessment
Pursuing Active BD activities underway Customer engagement, research
Capture Dedicated capture effort initiated Capture plan, solution development
Proposal RFP released, proposal in progress Writing, reviews, submission
Submitted Proposal submitted, awaiting decision Q&A, orals prep (if applicable)
Won/Lost Contract awarded or lost Lessons learned, debrief

3. Capture Phase: Positioning to Win

Capture is the intensive effort to position your company to win a specific, identified opportunity.

When Capture Begins: Typically when an opportunity is qualified and large/strategic enough to warrant dedicated resources.

Core Capture Activities:

Activity Purpose Deliverable
Capture Planning Define win strategy Capture plan
Customer Engagement Understand requirements and decision-makers Call reports, intel
Competitive Analysis Know your competitors and their strategies Competitive assessment
Solution Development Design a winning technical approach Solution brief
Teaming Assemble the right team Teaming agreements
Price-to-Win Estimate competitive pricing PTW analysis
Win Theme Development Craft compelling messages Win themes, discriminators

The Capture Manager Role: A capture manager is responsible for orchestrating all these activities and leading the team to a winning position before the RFP drops.

4. Proposal Phase: Responding to Win

The proposal phase begins when the solicitation is released and ends at submission.

Typical Proposal Timeline:

Day Activity
Day 1-3 RFP analysis, compliance matrix, kickoff
Day 4-14 Writing, content development
Day 15-21 Reviews (Pink Team, Red Team)
Day 22-28 Revisions, final reviews
Day 29-30 Production, final QC, submission

Note: Timelines vary significantly based on opportunity size and complexity.

Key Proposal Activities:

  • Compliance Matrix: Mapping RFP requirements to proposal sections
  • Technical Writing: Describing your solution and approach
  • Past Performance: Documenting relevant experience
  • Pricing: Developing competitive and compliant pricing
  • Reviews: Pink Team (outline/initial), Red Team (near-final)
  • Production: Formatting, page checks, submission

5. Gate Reviews: Go/No-Go Decisions

Gate reviews are formal decision points where leadership decides whether to continue pursuing an opportunity.

Common Gate Review Points:

Gate When Key Questions
Pursue Gate After identification Is this worth BD resources?
Capture Gate Before capture investment Can we win? Should we invest?
Bid Gate After RFP release Final decision to submit

Typical Go/No-Go Criteria:

Factor Green (Go) Yellow (Caution) Red (No-Go)
Capability Match Direct experience Adjacent experience No experience
Customer Relationship Known buyer Some contact No relationship
Competitive Position Incumbent or strong Competitive Longshot
Past Performance 3+ relevant refs 1-2 refs None
Price Competitiveness Can win on price Need to be creative Can’t compete
Resources Team available Stretched Not available
Set-Aside We qualify Need partner Don’t qualify

6. Teaming: Why It Matters

Most GovCon opportunities are won by teams, not individual companies. Teaming allows you to:

  • Fill capability gaps - Bring skills you don’t have in-house
  • Meet set-aside requirements - Partner with qualified small businesses
  • Strengthen past performance - Leverage partner experience
  • Increase geographic coverage - Add local presence
  • Reduce risk - Share workload and responsibility

Teaming Structures:

Structure What It Is When to Use
Prime/Sub One company leads, others support Most common; clear roles
Joint Venture (JV) New entity formed by two+ companies Large, long-term opportunities
Mentor-Protégé SBA program pairing large/small businesses Structured development relationships
CTA (Contractor Team Arrangement) Multiple primes coordinating Multiple award IDIQs

Finding Teaming Partners with DSBS:

The Dynamic Small Business Search (DSBS) at dsbs.sba.gov helps you find small business partners by: - Certification type (8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB) - NAICS code - Geographic location - Keywords

Teaming Partner Evaluation Criteria:

Factor What to Look For
Capability Do they do what we need?
Past Performance Do they have relevant experience?
Certifications Do they have needed set-aside status?
Reputation Are they reliable? Good to work with?
Financial Stability Can they perform without risk?
Culture Fit Will they work well with our team?

7. Your Role in BD/Capture

Even if you’re not in a formal BD or Capture role, you contribute to the process:

How Non-BD Staff Support Growth:

Your Role How You Help
Program/Project Staff Identify recompete opportunities; provide past performance data
Operations Spot delivery gaps competitors have; suggest improvements
Technical Staff Contribute to solution development; support proposals
Finance Support pricing; provide cost data
Contracts Review teaming agreements; ensure compliance
HR Support staffing plans; identify key personnel

The “Everyone Sells” Mentality: In the best GovCon companies, every employee understands that growth enables their job security and advancement.

AI Accelerator: Using AI for BD Process Support

AI tools can help you manage pipelines, evaluate teaming partners, and support capture activities. Use these prompts to work more effectively.

Prompt 1: Develop Go/No-Go Criteria

Use When: You need to create or refine qualification criteria for your company.

Help me develop go/no-go criteria for a company with these characteristics:

- Size: [SMALL/LARGE BUSINESS]
- Certifications: [YOUR CERTS]
- Core capabilities: [YOUR SERVICES]
- Target agencies: [AGENCIES]
- Typical contract size: [RANGE]
- Current capacity: [AVAILABLE RESOURCES]

Create a scoring matrix with:
1. Must-have criteria (deal breakers if not met)
2. Important criteria (strongly preferred)
3. Nice-to-have criteria (bonus points)
4. Red flags that should trigger no-go
5. Suggested scoring weights for each factor

Prompt 2: Evaluate Teaming Partner Fit

Use When: You’ve found a potential partner and need to assess fit.

We're evaluating a potential teaming partner for a [AGENCY] opportunity:

Our Company:
- Capabilities: [YOUR SERVICES]
- Certifications: [YOUR CERTS]
- Gap we need to fill: [WHAT WE NEED]

Potential Partner (from DSBS research):
- Name: [COMPANY]
- Capabilities: [THEIR SERVICES]
- Certifications: [THEIR CERTS]
- Size: [SMALL/LARGE]
- Location: [WHERE]

Help me evaluate:
1. Capability complementarity (do they fill our gaps?)
2. Certification value (do they help with set-asides?)
3. Potential conflicts (could they also compete against us?)
4. Questions to ask in initial teaming discussions
5. Red flags to watch for

Prompt 3: Draft Teaming Outreach Message

Use When: You want to reach out to a potential teaming partner.

I want to reach out to [COMPANY NAME] about potential teaming.

Context:
- Opportunity: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
- Agency: [AGENCY]
- Why we need a partner: [GAP TO FILL]
- What we bring: [OUR VALUE]
- How we found them: [DSBS, referral, etc.]

Draft a brief, professional outreach email that:
1. Introduces our company concisely
2. Explains why we're reaching out (specific opportunity or general)
3. Highlights potential mutual benefit
4. Requests a brief call to explore fit
5. Sounds professional but not overly formal

Keep it under 150 words.

Prompt 4: Create Pipeline Status Summary

Use When: You need to brief leadership on pipeline health.

Help me create a pipeline status briefing from this data:

[PASTE YOUR PIPELINE DATA - opportunities, stages, values, probabilities]

Create a summary that includes:
1. Total pipeline value and weighted value
2. Distribution across stages (are opportunities bunching?)
3. Near-term opportunities (decisions expected in 30/60/90 days)
4. Pipeline health assessment (is it growing, shrinking, aging?)
5. Recommendations for BD focus areas
6. Risks or concerns to flag

Format as a 1-page executive briefing.

Prompt 5: Prepare for Capture Kickoff

Use When: You’re transitioning an opportunity from BD to active capture.

We're kicking off capture for this opportunity:

- Opportunity: [TITLE]
- Agency: [AGENCY]
- Estimated Value: [AMOUNT]
- Expected RFP: [DATE]
- Competition: [KNOWN COMPETITORS]
- Our position: [INCUMBENT/CHALLENGER/NEW]

Help me prepare a capture kickoff agenda:
1. Key questions the team needs to answer
2. Initial win theme hypotheses to test
3. Customer intelligence gaps to fill
4. Competitive research priorities
5. Teaming decisions to make
6. First 30-day capture milestones

Pro Tips for AI-Assisted BD

Do Don’t
Use AI to draft teaming outreach emails Send AI-drafted emails without personalization
Get AI help structuring go/no-go criteria Let AI make final bid decisions
Use AI to summarize pipeline data Share confidential pipeline data with AI
Draft capture kickoff agendas with AI Skip human judgment on teaming decisions

Remember: BD is relationship-driven. AI helps with process and analysis, but winning is about people and positioning.

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded.

Question 1 (Recall)

What are the three main phases of the GovCon BD lifecycle?

  1. Marketing, Sales, Delivery
  2. BD, Capture, Proposal
  3. Research, Writing, Submission
  4. Planning, Execution, Closeout

Answer: B - BD (finding/qualifying opportunities), Capture (positioning to win), and Proposal (responding to solicitation).

Question 2 (Application)

Your company identifies a large opportunity 18 months before the expected RFP release. What phase should you be in?

  1. Proposal phase—start writing now
  2. BD/Capture phase—engage the customer and develop your solution
  3. Wait until the RFP is released
  4. Submission phase—prepare your documents

Answer: B - With 18 months lead time, you should be in active BD or early Capture, engaging the customer and positioning to win.

Question 3 (Scenario)

At a Capture Gate review, your team notes: strong capability match, good customer relationship, but the incumbent has held the contract for 10 years and the customer seems satisfied. What should this tell you?

  1. Automatic no-go—incumbents always win
  2. Proceed with caution—need a strong discriminator strategy to unseat the incumbent
  3. Full go—customer relationships don’t matter
  4. Ignore competitive analysis

Answer: B - Long-term incumbents have advantages, but they can be unseated. You need a clear strategy for why you’re a better choice.

Question 4 (Recall)

What tool would you use to find small business teaming partners by certification and NAICS code?

  1. SAM.gov
  2. SAM.gov Contract Data
  3. DSBS (Dynamic Small Business Search)
  4. USAspending

Answer: C - DSBS is specifically designed to search for small businesses by certification, NAICS code, location, and capabilities.

Question 5 (Application)

Your company is a large business pursuing a Total Small Business Set-Aside. What’s your only path to winning?

  1. Request the set-aside be removed
  2. Team with a qualified small business who primes, with your company as subcontractor
  3. Submit anyway and hope they waive the requirement
  4. Large businesses cannot participate in set-asides at all

Answer: B - You can support a small business prime as a subcontractor, bringing your capabilities while meeting set-aside requirements.

Question 6 (Scenario)

A program manager on your delivery team mentions the customer has been complaining about slow response times from the current vendor. How should this intelligence be used?

  1. Ignore it—delivery staff shouldn’t be involved in BD
  2. Share it with BD/Capture team as competitive intelligence for the recompete
  3. Immediately contact the contracting officer
  4. Post about it publicly

Answer: B - This is valuable competitive intelligence. When the contract recompetes, your team can emphasize responsiveness as a discriminator.

Key Terms

  • BD (Business Development): Activities to identify, qualify, and pursue opportunities
  • Capture: Focused effort to position for a specific opportunity
  • Pipeline: The collection of opportunities being tracked and pursued
  • Gate Review: Formal go/no-go decision point
  • Teaming: Partnering with other companies to pursue an opportunity
  • Prime: The lead contractor holding the government contract
  • Subcontractor: Company supporting the prime under a subcontract
  • Joint Venture: New legal entity formed by two or more companies
  • Win Themes: Key messages about why you should be selected
  • Discriminators: Factors that differentiate you from competitors
