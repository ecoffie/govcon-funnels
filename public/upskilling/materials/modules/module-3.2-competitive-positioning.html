Lesson Content

1. Why Competitive Intelligence Matters

In GovCon, you’re rarely the only company pursuing an opportunity. Understanding your competition allows you to:

  • Position your strengths against competitor weaknesses
  • Anticipate competitor strategies and counter them
  • Price competitively without leaving money on the table
  • Develop compelling win themes that resonate with evaluators
  • Make informed bid/no-bid decisions based on realistic win probability

Key Principle: You don’t need to be the best company—you need to be the best choice for this specific customer on this specific opportunity.

2. Types of Competitors

Competitor Type Definition Your Strategy
Incumbent Current contract holder Must prove you’re better; highlight transition risk they create
Direct Competitor Does exactly what you do Differentiate on execution, price, or approach
Adjacent Competitor Does similar work; could expand Emphasize your direct experience
New Entrant New to this market/customer Emphasize your established relationships and past performance
Teaming Competitor Potential partner who may also compete Watch for conflicts; consider teaming vs. competing

3. Researching Competitors with Free Tools

SAM.gov Contract Data: The Gold Mine for Competitive Intelligence

What You Can Learn from SAM.gov Contract Data: - Who won similar contracts - Contract values and durations - Contract types used (FFP, T&M, etc.) - Agency buying patterns - Competitor portfolio breadth

How to Research a Competitor in SAM.gov Contract Data: 1. Go to sam.gov and log in (free account required) 2. Navigate to “Contract Data” → “Contract Award Search” 3. Enter competitor name in “Vendor Name” field 4. Filter by date range, agency, or NAICS code 5. Analyze results

Key Data Points to Extract:

Data Point What It Tells You
Contract value Size of work they handle
Period of performance When it expires (recompete timing)
Contract type How they price/deliver
Contracting agency Where they have relationships
Number of modifications Scope growth or issues
NAICS codes What markets they’re in

Incumbent Analysis

The incumbent has significant advantages: - Known to the customer - Understands the environment - Has staff in place - Lower transition risk

But incumbents also have vulnerabilities: - Customer may be frustrated with performance - Costs may have grown over contract life - Staff may be stale; innovation lacking - Compliance issues may exist

How to Research the Incumbent: 1. SAM.gov Contract Data: Find original award, modifications, value 2. SAM.gov: Review opportunity history, any protests 3. Customer conversations: Listen for dissatisfaction signals 4. Industry intel: Talk to subs, former employees (ethically)

4. Developing Win Themes

Win themes are the core messages that answer: “Why should we be selected?”

Characteristics of Strong Win Themes: - Specific: Not generic (“we’re experienced”) - Relevant: Addresses what this customer cares about - Provable: Backed by evidence - Memorable: Evaluator remembers it

Win Theme Formula:

Because of [our unique capability/experience], we will deliver [specific benefit] that [addresses customer’s priority], as demonstrated by [proof point].

Example Win Themes:

Weak (Generic) Strong (Specific)
“We have experienced staff” “Our team includes 5 engineers with 10+ years at VA, reducing ramp-up time by 60%”
“We deliver quality” “Our ISO 9001-certified QMS has achieved 99.8% SLA compliance across 3 similar VA contracts”
“We’re cost-effective” “Our commercial cloud approach reduces infrastructure costs by 40% versus legacy alternatives”

5. Identifying Discriminators

Discriminators are factors that separate you from competitors. They answer: “Why us and not them?”

Types of Discriminators:

Type Examples
Technical Proprietary tool, unique methodology, patented technology
Personnel Key staff with rare expertise, former government leaders
Past Performance Specific similar work others can’t claim
Price Lower cost structure, innovative pricing model
Relationships Established customer knowledge, incumbent intelligence
Certifications Security clearances, ISO certifications, set-aside status
Location Local presence, reduced travel costs
Risk Reduction Transition approach, warranty, performance guarantees

Discriminator Test: Ask yourself: 1. Can competitors say the same thing? (If yes, not a discriminator) 2. Does the customer care about this? (If no, not valuable) 3. Can we prove it? (If no, don’t claim it)

Total Weighted Score 100%

Rating Scale: - 5 = Strong advantage - 4 = Slight advantage - 3 = Neutral/equal - 2 = Slight disadvantage - 1 = Strong disadvantage

7. Ghost and Counter Strategies

Ghosting: Indirectly highlighting competitor weaknesses without naming them.

Examples: - “Unlike approaches that rely on legacy technology, we use modern cloud-native solutions…” - “Our approach minimizes transition risk through our proven methodology…” - “We provide dedicated on-site staff, ensuring immediate response…”

Counter Strategies: Anticipating competitor claims and neutralizing them.

Competitor Likely Claim Your Counter
“We’re the incumbent” “Fresh perspective + proven transition methodology”
“We’re lowest price” “Total cost of ownership includes quality/risk”
“We have more staff” “Right-sized team with senior experts vs. junior staff”

8. Price-to-Win Concepts

Price-to-Win (PTW) is the estimated price needed to win, based on competitive analysis.

Factors Affecting PTW: - Incumbent’s likely price (based on current contract value) - Customer’s budget constraints - Market rates for similar work - Competitor cost structures - Evaluation weighting (is price heavily weighted?)

Basic PTW Approach: 1. Research incumbent contract value (SAM.gov Contract Data) 2. Estimate competitor pricing using public data 3. Consider customer budget signals 4. Factor in evaluation criteria weights 5. Determine if you can be competitive

Note: Detailed PTW analysis is a specialized skill typically done by capture/pricing professionals. This module introduces the concept.

Pro Tips for AI-Assisted Competitive Positioning

Do Don’t
Use AI to brainstorm win themes Use AI-generated themes word-for-word
Get AI help structuring SWOT analysis Share competitor confidential information
Draft ghosting language with AI Write anything negative about competitors
Analyze pricing scenarios with AI Let AI set your actual price

Remember: Great competitive positioning comes from deep customer knowledge. AI helps structure your thinking, but winning insights come from customer conversations and research.

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded.

Question 1 (Recall)

What is the primary source for researching competitor contract awards?

  1. LinkedIn
  2. SAM.gov Contract Data
  3. Company websites
  4. Trade publications

Answer: B - SAM.gov Contract Data contains official government contract award data for all contracts over $10,000. (Note: This replaced FPDS.gov in February 2026.)

Question 2 (Application)

You’re researching an incumbent and find they’ve received 12 modifications to their contract over 3 years, with total value increasing 80%. What might this indicate?

  1. The incumbent is doing excellent work
  2. Possible scope creep, cost growth, or performance issues worth investigating
  3. Nothing significant
  4. The government is satisfied

Answer: B - While modifications can be positive (additional work), excessive modifications often indicate scope creep, underestimation, or issues. It’s worth investigating further.

Question 3 (Scenario)

Your win theme is: “We’re a great company with experienced staff who deliver quality work.” What’s wrong with this?

  1. Nothing—it’s a strong theme
  2. It’s too generic; competitors can say the same thing
  3. It’s too specific
  4. It mentions staff

Answer: B - This is generic. A strong win theme is specific, relevant, provable, and memorable. Anyone can claim to be “great” with “experienced staff.”

Question 4 (Application)

You identify that the incumbent has a weakness: their staff turnover has been high, causing knowledge loss. How would you “ghost” this in your proposal?

  1. Write: “Unlike the incumbent who has high turnover…”
  2. Write: “Our approach ensures continuity through retained institutional knowledge and a 95% staff retention rate…”
  3. Directly criticize the incumbent in your executive summary
  4. Ignore it

Answer: B - Ghosting highlights competitor weaknesses indirectly by emphasizing your corresponding strength, without naming them.

Question 5 (Recall)

What is a “discriminator” in competitive positioning?

  1. A factor that unfairly advantages one competitor
  2. A factor that separates you from competitors that the customer values
  3. The lowest price
  4. Past performance only

Answer: B - A discriminator is something that differentiates you from competitors AND that the customer cares about. It answers “why you and not them.”

Question 6 (Scenario)

You’re assessing competitors for an IT modernization contract. You rate yourself as follows: Technical=4, Past Performance=3, Price=2. The evaluation weights are Technical 30%, Past Performance 40%, Price 30%. What’s your biggest concern?

  1. Technical score—it needs to be higher
  2. Past Performance—it’s weighted highest and you’re only neutral
  3. Price—you’re at a disadvantage on a significant factor
  4. Both B and C are concerns

Answer: D - Past Performance is weighted 40% and you’re only neutral. Price is weighted 30% and you’re at a disadvantage. Both need attention.
