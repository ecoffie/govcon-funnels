Lesson Content

1. Introduction to SAM.gov Opportunities

SAM.gov is the official government website for finding federal contract opportunities. All opportunities over $25,000 must be posted here.

What You’ll Find: - Active solicitations (RFPs, RFQs) - Pre-solicitation notices - Sources Sought / RFI - Award notices - Justifications for sole source

Why This Matters for Your Role: Even if you’re not in BD, knowing how to find and recognize opportunities helps you: - Spot relevant opportunities in your area of expertise - Understand what your company is pursuing - Contribute to qualification discussions

2. Types of Opportunities

Understanding the different opportunity types helps you know what action is needed:

Type Full Name What It Means Action Required
RFP Request for Proposal Formal solicitation requiring full proposal Submit proposal by deadline
RFQ Request for Quotation Request for pricing on defined requirements Submit quote
RFI Request for Information Market research - agency gathering info Respond to influence requirements
Sources Sought Sources Sought Notice Agency looking for capable vendors Respond to get on their radar
Pre-Sol Pre-Solicitation Notice Heads up that RFP/RFQ is coming Prepare, watch for solicitation
Award Award Notice Contract has been awarded Research winner, learn from loss

3. Navigating SAM.gov

Accessing Contract Opportunities: 1. Go to https://sam.gov 2. Click “Contract Opportunities” in the main menu 3. Use the search bar and filters

Key Search Filters:

Filter Purpose Example
Keyword Search title and description “IT support services”
Notice Type Filter by opportunity type “Solicitation”
NAICS Code Filter by industry “541512”
PSC Code Filter by product/service “D302”
Set-Aside Filter by small business type “8(a)”, “SDVOSB”
Place of Performance Filter by location “Virginia”
Agency Filter by contracting agency “Department of Defense”
Posted Date Filter by when posted “Last 30 days”
Response Date Filter by deadline “Active only”

4. Reading an Opportunity Notice

Key Information to Extract:

Field What to Look For
Title Brief description of requirement
Solicitation Number Unique identifier for tracking
Notice Type RFP, RFQ, Sources Sought, etc.
Posted Date When it was published
Response Date Deadline for submission
Set-Aside Small business restriction (if any)
NAICS Code Industry classification
Place of Performance Where work will be done
Contracting Office Who is buying
Point of Contact Who to ask questions
Description Scope of work summary
Attachments Full solicitation documents

5. Opportunity Qualification

Not every opportunity is worth pursuing. Use these criteria to qualify:

Basic Qualification Questions:

Question Why It Matters
Do we do this work? Core capability match
Can we meet the set-aside? Eligibility requirement
Do we have relevant past performance? Evaluation factor
Is the timeline realistic? Can we respond in time?
Is the contract size right for us? Resource availability
Do we have/want a presence in this location? Place of performance
Do we know this customer? Relationship advantage

Quick Qualification Matrix:

Factor Green (Go) Yellow (Caution) Red (No-Go)
Capability Direct match Adjacent capability No experience
Set-Aside We qualify Need partner Don’t qualify
Past Performance 3+ relevant 1-2 relevant None
Timeline 30+ days 15-30 days <15 days
Customer Known Some contact No relationship

6. Setting Up Saved Searches

Why Save Searches: - Get email notifications for new opportunities - Don’t miss relevant postings - Save time on daily searching

How to Create a Saved Search: 1. Build your search using filters 2. Click “Save Search” 3. Name your search (e.g., “VA IT Services”) 4. Set notification frequency (daily recommended) 5. Confirm email settings

Recommended Saved Searches: - By target agency + your NAICS codes - By set-aside type you qualify for - By keywords specific to your services - By geographic region

AI Accelerator: Using AI for Opportunity Discovery

AI tools can help you quickly analyze opportunities, understand requirements, and make qualification decisions. Use these prompts to work smarter.

Prompt 1: Summarize a SAM.gov Opportunity

Use When: You find an opportunity and need a quick summary for your team.

I found this opportunity on SAM.gov. Please summarize it for a quick
internal review:

[PASTE THE OPPORTUNITY DESCRIPTION FROM SAM.GOV]

Provide:
1. A 2-3 sentence executive summary
2. Key requirements (bullets)
3. Important dates and deadlines
4. Set-aside type and eligibility requirements
5. Initial qualification assessment (what do we need to be competitive?)

Prompt 2: Analyze RFI or Sources Sought

Use When: You need to decide whether to respond to market research.

This Sources Sought notice was posted on SAM.gov:

[PASTE THE SOURCES SOUGHT OR RFI TEXT]

Help me understand:
1. What is the government trying to learn?
2. What information should we include in our response?
3. How can we use this response to influence the eventual RFP?
4. What questions should we ask the contracting officer?
5. Should we respond? What's the strategic value?

- Geography: [PREFERRED LOCATIONS]

Recommend:
1. 3-5 saved searches we should create
2. Keywords to include/exclude

4. Red flags that indicate poor fit
5. Any filters we might be missing

Pro Tips for AI-Assisted Opportunity Discovery

Do Don’t
Paste full opportunity text for accurate analysis Ask AI to find opportunities (use SAM.gov)
Use AI to summarize long solicitations quickly Let AI make final go/no-go decisions
Get AI help prioritizing your pipeline Skip reading the actual RFP
Draft qualification memos with AI assistance Rely on AI for deadline calculations

Remember: SAM.gov is your source of truth. AI helps you analyze faster, but always verify details against the official posting.

Applied Exercise: Build Your Opportunity Log

Scenario: Your manager asks you to identify 10 potential opportunities that might be relevant to your company’s capabilities.

Instructions:

Part 1: Define Your Search Criteria

Before searching, identify: - 2-3 NAICS codes relevant to your company - Target agencies (or use “all”) - Set-aside types your company qualifies for - Geographic focus (if any)

Part 2: Search SAM.gov

  1. Go to https://sam.gov/content/opportunities
  2. Apply your filters
  3. Review the results
  4. Identify 10 opportunities that might be relevant

Part 3: Document Each Opportunity

For each of the 10 opportunities, record: - Solicitation number - Title - Agency - Notice type - Response deadline - Set-aside type - Estimated value (if listed) - Quick qualification assessment

Part 4: Create a Saved Search

  1. Build a search for your target market
  2. Save it with notifications enabled
  3. Note the search name in your log

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded.

Question 1 (Recall)

What is the difference between an RFP and an RFI?

  1. RFP is for products, RFI is for services
  2. RFP is a formal solicitation requiring a proposal; RFI is market research gathering information
  3. RFP is only for large contracts, RFI is for small purchases
  4. There is no difference

Answer: B - An RFP requires a formal proposal response. An RFI is the government gathering information before they create a solicitation.

Question 2 (Application)

You find an opportunity that matches your capabilities, but it’s set aside for 8(a) companies and your company doesn’t have that certification. What should you do?

  1. Submit a proposal anyway
  2. Ignore the opportunity
  3. Consider teaming with an 8(a) company as a subcontractor
  4. Request the set-aside be removed

Answer: C - If you can’t prime, you might be able to support an 8(a) prime as a subcontractor.

Question 3 (Scenario)

You see a “Sources Sought” notice for services your company provides. The notice asks for capability statements. What is the government trying to accomplish?

  1. They’re awarding a contract to whoever responds
  2. They’re gathering market information to plan a future procurement
  3. They’re required to post this but won’t read responses
  4. They’ve already selected a winner

Answer: B - Sources Sought notices are market research. Your response helps shape the requirement and shows you’re a capable vendor.

Question 4 (Application)

An opportunity has a response deadline of 5 days from now. Your company has never worked with this agency and has no proposal draft. What’s the likely qualification?

  1. Green - submit anyway
  2. Yellow - possible with overtime
  3. Red - not enough time to submit quality response
  4. Green - short deadlines mean less competition

Answer: C - 5 days with no relationship or draft is typically not enough time for a quality response. Better to track for recompete.

Question 5 (Recall)

Where do you find the detailed requirements and submission instructions for an RFP?

  1. In the notice title
  2. In the attachments section of the opportunity
  3. By calling the contracting officer
  4. On the agency’s main website

Answer: B - The full solicitation documents (SOW, instructions, evaluation criteria) are in the attachments.
