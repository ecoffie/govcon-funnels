Lesson Content

1. Why Customer Engagement Matters

In GovCon, contracts are won long before the RFP is released. Building relationships with government customers allows you to:

  • Understand their pain points before requirements are written
  • Shape requirements through legitimate market research participation
  • Build trust so evaluators recognize your company
  • Identify teaming opportunities through agency introductions
  • Stay informed about upcoming procurements

Key Principle: The best BD happens before the solicitation drops.

2. Key Customer Touchpoints

Federal agencies have multiple entry points for vendor engagement:

Touchpoint Who They Are What They Do When to Engage
OSDBU Office of Small & Disadvantaged Business Utilization Advocate for small business participation Early/always
Contracting Office Contracting Officers (COs), Contract Specialists Issue solicitations, award contracts After RFI/Sources Sought
Program Office Mission owners, end users Define requirements, manage programs Early market research
Technical Staff Engineers, analysts, subject matter experts Evaluate technical solutions Industry days, demos

3. Types of Customer Engagement Events

Industry Days

What: Government-hosted events where agencies brief upcoming procurements and vendors can ask questions.

Purpose: - Government shares acquisition plans - Vendors learn about requirements - Networking with government and potential partners - Q&A with contracting and program staff

How to Find: - SAM.gov (filter by “Special Notice” or “Presolicitation”) - Agency OSDBU calendars - Agency procurement forecast pages

What to Do: - Research the agency and opportunity beforehand - Prepare 2-3 thoughtful questions - Bring business cards and capability statements - Network with other vendors (potential teammates)

Capability Briefings (Cap Briefs)

What: Short presentations (15-30 minutes) to government officials about your company’s capabilities.

Purpose: - Introduce your company to potential customers - Educate them on solutions they may not know exist - Build relationships before opportunities arise

How to Request: 1. Contact the agency OSDBU 2. Request a capability briefing with relevant program or contracting offices 3. Prepare a focused, non-salesy presentation 4. Follow up appropriately

Best Practices: - Focus on solving their problems, not selling your services - Keep it short and relevant - Bring something useful (industry insights, not just a sales pitch) - Don’t ask about specific opportunities (that’s inappropriate)

Vendor Outreach Sessions

What: Agency-hosted sessions for small businesses or specific vendor categories.

Purpose: - Agencies fulfill their small business outreach requirements - Vendors learn about the agency’s mission and needs - Matchmaking between primes and small businesses

Where to Find: - OSDBU event calendars - Agency small business conferences - Apex Accelerator announcements

4. OSDBU: Your Gateway to Agencies

Every major federal agency has an Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization (OSDBU).

What OSDBU Does for You: - Hosts matchmaking events - Connects small businesses with program offices - Provides guidance on agency procurement - Tracks agency small business goals - Advocates for small business participation

How to Engage: 1. Find the agency’s OSDBU contact page 2. Attend their events and webinars 3. Request introduction to relevant program offices 4. Ask about upcoming forecast opportunities 5. Register for their vendor database (if applicable)

5. Preparing for Customer Engagement

Before Any Engagement:

Step Action Why It Matters
1 Research the agency’s mission Show you understand their world
2 Review recent contract awards (SAM.gov Contract Data) Know what they buy
3 Check their procurement forecast Know what’s coming
4 Prepare your elevator pitch 30-second company overview
5 Bring capability statement Leave-behind document
6 Prepare questions Show genuine interest

Your Elevator Pitch Formula:

“We’re [Company Name], a [business type/certification] company that helps federal agencies [solve specific problem]. We’ve supported [relevant customers] with [relevant work]. We’re particularly interested in [their mission area].”

Example: > “We’re TechForward Solutions, a service-disabled veteran-owned small business that helps federal agencies modernize legacy IT systems. We’ve supported the VA and DoD with cloud migration and cybersecurity. We’re particularly interested in DHS’s IT modernization initiatives.”

6. Rules of Engagement (Ethics & Compliance)

Federal procurement has strict rules about vendor-government interactions. Know the boundaries.

Appropriate Engagement: - Attending public industry days - Responding to RFIs and Sources Sought notices - Requesting OSDBU capability briefings - Asking general questions about agency mission - Sharing industry insights (not proprietary) - Networking at public events

Inappropriate Engagement: - Asking about specific pending source selections - Offering gifts or meals to government employees - Attempting to influence evaluation criteria inappropriately - Discussing competitor bids or pricing - Contacting evaluators during source selection - Providing false or misleading information

The Procurement Integrity Act (FAR 3.104) prohibits: - Obtaining contractor bid or proposal information - Obtaining source selection information - Improper contact with procurement officials

Rule of Thumb: If it feels like you’re trying to gain an unfair advantage, don’t do it.

AI Accelerator: Using AI for Customer Engagement

AI tools can help you prepare for customer meetings, craft elevator pitches, and develop engagement strategies. Use these prompts to be better prepared.

Prompt 1: Research an Agency Before Engagement

Use When: You have a meeting or event with a specific agency.

I have a meeting with representatives from [AGENCY NAME] next week.
Help me prepare by providing:

1. The agency's core mission and strategic priorities
2. Recent news or initiatives I should know about
3. Common pain points agencies like this typically face
4. Questions I should ask to understand their needs
5. Topics to avoid or be careful discussing
6. How to position a company that provides [YOUR SERVICES]

I'll verify current details on the agency website.

Prompt 2: Craft Your Elevator Pitch

Use When: You need a concise company introduction for networking.

Help me create a 30-second elevator pitch for government customers.

Our company:
- Name: [COMPANY NAME]
- Size/Certifications: [e.g., SDVOSB, 8(a), small business]
- Core services: [WHAT YOU DO]
- Target agencies: [WHO YOU SERVE]
- Key differentiator: [WHAT MAKES YOU DIFFERENT]
- Recent win or achievement: [PROOF POINT]

Create 3 versions:
1. General networking version
2. Version for [SPECIFIC AGENCY]
3. Version emphasizing our [CERTIFICATION] status

Keep each under 30 seconds when spoken aloud.

Prompt 3: Prepare Capability Briefing Talking Points

Use When: You’re preparing for a capability briefing with an agency.

I'm preparing a capability briefing for [AGENCY NAME]'s OSDBU.
Our company provides [YOUR SERVICES].

Help me develop:
1. An opening hook that connects to their mission
2. 3-5 key capability points to emphasize
3. A relevant past performance example to mention
4. 2-3 questions to ask them (that are appropriate)
5. A clear call-to-action for next steps
6. Topics to avoid (what NOT to discuss)

Remember: This is educational, not a sales pitch.

Prompt 4: Draft Industry Day Questions

Use When: You’re attending an industry day and want to ask smart questions.

I'm attending an industry day for this upcoming procurement:

[PASTE INDUSTRY DAY ANNOUNCEMENT OR PROCUREMENT DESCRIPTION]

Our company provides [YOUR SERVICES] and we're interested in pursuing this.

Help me prepare:
1. 3-5 thoughtful questions to ask during Q&A
2. Questions that show we understand their challenges
3. Questions that might reveal evaluation priorities
4. Networking conversation starters for other attendees
5. What to listen for during the briefing

Questions should be appropriate (not asking for source selection info).

Prompt 5: Follow-Up Email After Customer Meeting

Use When: You need to send a professional follow-up after engagement.

I just met with [NAME/TITLE] from [AGENCY] at [EVENT/MEETING TYPE].

Key discussion points:
- [TOPIC 1]
- [TOPIC 2]
- [THEIR INTEREST OR PAIN POINT]

Help me draft a follow-up email that:
1. Thanks them appropriately (not overly effusive)
2. References our specific conversation
3. Offers something of value (insight, resource, connection)
4. Suggests a clear next step
5. Remains professional and compliant with ethics rules

Keep it concise - 3-4 short paragraphs maximum.

Pro Tips for AI-Assisted Customer Engagement

Do Don’t
Use AI to prepare talking points and research Let AI write emails you send without review
Practice elevator pitches generated by AI Use AI-generated content word-for-word
Get AI help with follow-up email drafts Ask AI about specific procurement details
Research agency priorities with AI assistance Skip verifying info on agency websites

Remember: AI helps you prepare, but authentic relationships come from genuine human interaction. Use AI to be better prepared, not to replace personal connection.

7. Documenting Customer Engagement

Track all customer interactions in a structured format:

What to Document: - Date and type of engagement - Agency and office contacted - Names and titles of government contacts - Topics discussed - Follow-up actions committed - Intelligence gathered - Next steps

Why Document: - Builds institutional knowledge - Supports capture planning - Enables team coordination - Demonstrates relationship history in proposals - Tracks commitments and follow-ups

Knowledge Check (Self-Assessment)

Purpose: These questions help reinforce your learning. They are NOT graded.

Question 1 (Recall)

What does OSDBU stand for?

  1. Office of Small and Diversified Business Utilization
  2. Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization
  3. Office of Strategic and Defense Business Units
  4. Office of Supply and Demand Business Understanding

Answer: B - Office of Small and Disadvantaged Business Utilization. Every major federal agency has one.

Question 2 (Application)

You want to introduce your company to a federal agency before any specific opportunities are announced. What is the most appropriate first step?

  1. Email the contracting officer directly
  2. Contact the agency’s OSDBU to request a capability briefing
  3. Wait for an RFP to be released
  4. Send unsolicited proposals to program offices

Answer: B - The OSDBU is designed to help vendors connect with the agency. They can facilitate appropriate introductions.

Question 3 (Scenario)

You’re at an industry day, and a government program manager mentions they’re frustrated with their current contractor’s slow response times. How should you respond?

  1. Immediately criticize the competitor and pitch your faster services
  2. Ask probing questions to understand their challenges, then follow up appropriately after the event
  3. Ask who the current contractor is so you can prepare to compete
  4. Offer to meet privately to discuss how to replace the incumbent

Answer: B - Listen, understand their pain points, and follow up through appropriate channels. Don’t criticize competitors or try to circumvent proper procurement processes.

Question 4 (Application)

Which of the following is NOT an appropriate topic for a capability briefing?

  1. Your company’s relevant experience and certifications
  2. Industry trends and best practices
  3. Asking about evaluation criteria for an upcoming RFP
  4. How your solutions have helped similar agencies

Answer: C - Capability briefings are for general education, not for gaining procurement-sensitive information about specific opportunities.

Question 5 (Recall)

What federal regulation governs procurement integrity and prohibits improper contact with procurement officials?

  1. FAR Part 15
  2. FAR 3.104 (Procurement Integrity Act)
  3. FAR Part 52
  4. FAR Part 19

Answer: B - FAR 3.104 implements the Procurement Integrity Act, which prohibits obtaining source selection information and improper contacts.

Question 6 (Scenario)

Your company holds an 8(a) certification. You find that an agency’s OSDBU is hosting a “Small Business Matchmaking Event” next month. What should you do?

  1. Skip it—matchmaking events are only for primes seeking subs
  2. Register and attend prepared with capability statements and questions
  3. Wait until a specific RFP is released
  4. Send a representative who doesn’t need preparation

Answer: B - Matchmaking events are valuable for meeting both government contacts and potential teaming partners. Come prepared.
