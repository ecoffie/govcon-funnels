Objection Handling

“We already have BD training”

Response: “Great—this isn’t for your BD team. This is for everyone else. Your operations staff, PMs, and delivery teams interact with customers daily but probably don’t know how to recognize opportunity signals or support capture efforts. This fills that gap.”

“Our people don’t have time”

Response: “The program is 5-6 hours per week for 4 weeks—comparable to one day of training per week. And the skills are immediately applicable. Most participants start identifying opportunities during the first week.”

“$3,999 per employee is expensive”

Response: “Consider this: one federal contract is worth millions. If training five employees helps you win one additional contract, the ROI is 100x or more. Plus, Section 127 makes it tax-advantaged—your effective cost is closer to $3,159.”

“We’re a small company—we can’t afford this”

Response: “Small companies often need this most. You probably don’t have a dedicated BD team, so everyone needs to contribute to growth. Training 3-5 key people gives you BD capability without hiring specialists.”

“Can’t we just train people internally?”

Response: “You could, but do you have someone with the time and expertise to build and deliver a structured program? Our curriculum is built on industry standards (NCMA, PMI), uses free public tools, and comes with a professional facilitator. Your team can focus on winning work.”

“What if people leave after we train them?”

Response: “Two points: First, training improves retention—employees who feel invested in are more loyal. Second, you can use a service commitment agreement requiring repayment if they leave within 12-24 months. We provide templates.”

Discovery Questions

Identify Pain Points

  1. “How does your BD team currently get intelligence from delivery staff?”
  2. “When was the last time an operations person surfaced an opportunity that turned into a pursuit?”
  3. “How early do you typically learn about recompete opportunities?”
  4. “What happens when a BD person leaves? Who fills the gap?”
  5. “How confident are your non-BD staff in customer conversations?”

Qualify the Opportunity

  1. “How many employees work on federal contracts?”
  2. “What’s your annual federal revenue?”
  3. “How many contracts are up for recompete in the next 2 years?”
  4. “Do you have an educational assistance program (Section 127)?”
  5. “Who makes training decisions—HR, BD leadership, or executives?”

Establish Value

  1. “What would one additional contract win be worth to you?”
  2. “What’s the cost of losing a recompete due to poor preparation?”
  3. “How much do you spend on external BD consultants?”
  4. “What’s the turnover cost when a BD person leaves?”

The Sales Process

Step 1: Discovery Call (30 min)

Goal: Understand their situation and qualify

Agenda: 1. Introductions (2 min) 2. Their current BD structure and challenges (10 min) 3. Program overview and fit (10 min) 4. Q&A (5 min) 5. Next steps (3 min)

Exit Criteria: Identified 3+ pain points; know decision maker; timeline established

Step 2: Presentation (45 min)

Goal: Demonstrate value and address objections

Agenda: 1. Recap discovery findings (5 min) 2. Program deep dive (20 min) 3. ROI discussion (10 min) 4. Objection handling (5 min) 5. Proposal preview (5 min)

Exit Criteria: Stakeholder buy-in; clear next steps to proposal

Step 3: Proposal & Close

Goal: Formalize agreement

Proposal Contents: - Executive summary - Program description - Pricing (based on volume) - Timeline and logistics - Section 127 documentation - Terms and conditions

Close Timeline: 2-4 weeks from proposal to enrollment
