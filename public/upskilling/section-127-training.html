What is IRS Section 127?

IRS Section 127 (Internal Revenue Code §127) allows employers to create Educational Assistance Programs (EAPs) that provide tax-free educational benefits to employees. Enacted to encourage employer-sponsored education, Section 127 is one of the most underused tax advantages available to businesses today.

When an employer establishes a qualifying EAP, educational assistance payments are excluded from the employee's gross income — meaning neither the employer nor the employee pays taxes on the benefit.

Key Facts About Section 127

  • ✓ Up to $5,250 per year per employee
  • ✓ Tax-free for both employer AND employee
  • ✓ No payroll tax (FICA, FUTA) on the benefit
  • ✓ Covers tuition, fees, books, supplies, and equipment
  • ✓ Employer must have a written Educational Assistance Plan

How Section 127 Works for GovCon Training

Using Section 127 for employee training is straightforward. Here is the process from start to finish:

1

Establish a Written EAP

Your company creates a written Educational Assistance Plan that meets IRS requirements. This is a standard document your HR or legal team can draft.

2

Enroll in Qualifying Training

Employer pays for qualifying training — like our BD Foundation Certificate program — on behalf of the employee.

3

Exclude from Gross Income

Up to $5,250 is excluded from the employee's gross income. No federal income tax, no FICA, no FUTA on the benefit.

4

Deduct as Business Expense

The employer deducts the full training cost as a business expense, reducing taxable income for the company.

Is Your Company Eligible?

Most employers — including small businesses, mid-size GovCon firms, and large primes — can take advantage of Section 127. Here are the IRS requirements:

  • 📋 Written plan required — You must have a written Educational Assistance Plan (EAP) on file. This is a standard document, not a complex legal filing.
  • ⚖ Non-discriminatory — The plan cannot favor highly compensated employees. It must be available to a broad class of employees.
  • 📈 5% owner limit — No more than 5% of total benefits paid during the year can go to shareholders or owners who hold more than 5% of the company.
  • 📄 Separate plan — The EAP must be a separate written plan, not combined with other benefit plans like cafeteria plans.
Most companies can set up an EAP in a few days. We can walk your HR team through the process and provide a sample plan template as part of enrollment.
