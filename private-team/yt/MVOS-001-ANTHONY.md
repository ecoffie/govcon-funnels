## 1 · Story DNA™ (from the transcript)
| Gene | |
|---|---|
| **WHO** | Anthony Closson — USAF Staff Sergeant (2005–09), a "wire dog" / cyber-transport airman turned program manager running BRAC IT requirements at AFDW. Working-class railroad family; grew up with no healthcare or dentist. |
| **BEFORE** | The airman *on the other side of the table* — evaluating proposals from Lockheed and the big primes, watching the money go by. |
| **TRIGGER** | "It just clicked" — he could be on the *other* side, owning the company that supports the mission. Founded Colossal Contracting, 2009, as the economy tanked, with "zero dollars" and a staff sergeant's last paycheck. |
| **CONFLICT** | Saved $100K, walked into a bank, asked for a **$10,000 credit card — denied.** "That's how bad the economy was." Getting anyone to back him financially was the hardest thing he faced. |
| **DISCOVERY** | Become a *great subcontractor first* ("the lessons you learn being a good subcontractor prepare you for a good prime") **+ Joint Ventures / Mentor-Protégé — inspired by Steven Koprince (Ep 81).** Get out of your comfort zone; make the connections. |
| **TRANSFORMATION** | Colossal supports the **White House Communications Agency**, DHS, SOCOM, USAF, DOL, VA — IT + professional services, CONUS/OCONUS. Employees with the healthcare and benefits he never had. |
| **LEGACY** | Give back to the veteran and service-disabled community; provide for employees and their families; mentor the next founders. "You can't just spend your life taking." |

**Belief Shift:** *Most people think you should chase the prime spot and the big money first. Actually, mastering subcontracting first is what earns you the prime.*
**Opportunity:** Subcontractor-first + JV/Mentor-Protégé (Opportunity DNA: hidden ✓ accessible ✓ repeatable ✓ transformational ✓ teachable ✓).
**Emotion:** Hope (with a spine of vindication).
**CTA:** Find one prime to subcontract under this month; study the Mentor-Protégé Program.

## 6 · Documentary structure
Cold Open (the $10K denial) → Conflict (broke, 2009, no backing) → Origin (staff sergeant evaluating primes' proposals) → Turning Point (subcontract-first + the Koprince JV epiphany) → Framework (how to be a great sub → prime) → Results (White House, SOCOM, DHS) → Lessons (banker, JV, differentiate) → CTA (find one prime to sub under; study MPP).

## 7 · Retention plan
Front-load the denial in the first 15 seconds (open on `BANK SAID NO`). Plant the "White House" payoff as an open loop early, deliver it at the midpoint. Use the "bankers came back in year two" reversal as the ¾ retention spike. Never explain subcontracting as theory — always through his arc.
