═══ COLD OPEN — CHALLENGE THE BELIEF ═══

Every industry has a version of the same lie: before anyone will pay you, you need the credential. The license. The certification. The special badge that finally makes you legitimate.

So you stall. Six months. A year. Two years — filling out paperwork instead of finding customers, waiting for someone to hand you permission to make money.

I work in the most extreme version of that market there is: selling to the U.S. government. Three-quarters of a trillion dollars a year, and everyone — everyone — believes that money is locked behind certifications. Woman-owned. Veteran-owned. 8(a). HUBZone. If the permission-slip belief is true anywhere, it should be true here.

So listen to what actually happened to four business owners I interviewed. A first-time bidder. An appraiser. A 27-year-old with a dumpster contract. A lighting guy. They won at every level of government — a school program, a city, a federal agency, an Army base.

Not one of them held a certification when they won. Four people. Never met each other. Listen.


═══ CLIP 1 — SARAH BINETTE ═══

Start with Sarah Binette. Stay-at-home mom, meal-prep business. And she was doing exactly what everyone says to do — buried in the paperwork for not one but three certifications. Listen to where she was when she stopped waiting.

[CLIP: Sarah — "I was at that point going for my WOSB, my EDWOSB… my kitchen's in a HUBZone… and he was like, don't wait on certifications. You've got to just start and take action." @locate-on-tape · Ep 177]

So she picked a real contract instead of a form. Her first bid — the first one she ever submitted in her life — was 1.5 million breakfast bags for a school nutrition program, head to head against the big preferred brand.

[CLIP: Sarah — "That was the first time I've ever bid on a contract. I won it as a prime for 1,500,000 breakfast bags against a preferred brand without designation… it was this David and Goliath moment." @locate-on-tape · Ep 177]

First bid ever. Beat the name brand. Zero certifications.


═══ CLIP 2 — ASTRELAIN JOSUE ═══

Maybe that's a fluke. So here's Astrelain Josue — runs an appraisal company in South Florida. One night he updated the keywords on his free government profile. The next day — the next DAY — a federal buyer emailed him an opportunity. He lost the first two. Won the third. He's now won around fifteen federal contracts with the USDA and is part of the team on a ten-year, billion-dollar contract. And I asked him the question straight up:

[CLIP: Eric — "When they sent you that, did you have a certification?" / Astrelain — "No, I didn't have a certification… federal certifications, HUBZone, or 8(a) — nothing." @locate-on-tape · Ep 248]

Nothing. The buyer found HIM — off keywords he typed into a free profile at midnight.


═══ CLIP 3 — DEVOSKI BOYD ═══

Devoski Boyd. Twenty-seven years old, Montgomery, Alabama. He's got the waste contract at an Army training base — real federal money, locked in through 2028. Here's the exchange, word for word:

[CLIP: Eric — "Do you have any type of certifications?" / Devoski — "Certifications? I mean… self-cert, self-cert, self-cert." / Eric — "You just registered in SAM?" / Devoski — "Man, I registered in SAM's, and I followed the blueprint." @locate-on-tape · Ep 249]

Self-cert means he checked a box saying "I'm a small business." That's it. That's the whole credential behind a multi-year contract with the United States Army.


═══ CLIP 4 — CHRIS MOORE ═══

And Chris Moore — LED lighting, San Antonio. Went from nothing to three government awards in one single week: city parks, the airport. And here's the part that should end this debate, because I asked him how they could even GIVE him the work without a certification:

[CLIP: Chris — "Honestly, the first one, parks and recreation — they never even asked me anything about any certification. Two out of the first three, they did not ask for anything. It was just: can you supply?" @locate-on-tape · Ep 253]

They never even asked. And his verdict after all three wins:

[CLIP: Chris — "Certifications are not the end-all, be-all… it's relationships, and what are your actual capabilities. Not what it says on paper." @locate-on-tape · Ep 253]


═══ NAME THE PATTERN ═══

Four owners. Four different industries — food, appraisals, waste, lighting. Four different levels of government, from a school district all the way up to the Army.

Nobody asked for the certification. The buyer's question was never "are you certified?" It was "can you do the work?"

Now — I'm not telling you certifications are worthless. Some money really is set aside, and you should collect them eventually. But the order is backwards. You get certified WHILE you're winning — not instead of winning. Like Sarah heard me say: go get your certifications for what? First, prove you can do the work.

If the most bureaucratic customer on planet Earth doesn't need your permission slip before it pays you — your market doesn't either.
