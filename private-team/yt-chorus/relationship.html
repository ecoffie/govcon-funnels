═══ COLD OPEN — CHALLENGE THE BELIEF ═══

Everybody thinks winning a government contract comes down to two things. Finding the right bid on SAM.gov, and writing the best proposal. Turn it in, cross your fingers, hope you win.

So here's what surprised me. I've sat down with contractor after contractor — different industries, different states, people who have never met each other — and I asked them one question. How did you actually win?

And almost none of them led with the bidding. They told me the contract was basically won before it ever posted. Not by the proposal. By the relationship.

Three of them. Let me show you.


═══ CLIP 1 — EUGENE TINKER ═══

First, Eugene Tinker. Two companies, eighty-five federal contracts. When I asked him what actually wins the work, he said it in one line.

[CLIP: Eugene Tinker — "Proposals win new opportunities. Relationships win new contracts." @locate-on-tape · Ep 158]

Read that again. Proposals win you the opportunity. The relationship wins you the contract. His very first award? A direct award at 11:59 p.m. on the last night of the fiscal year — no proposal writing at all. His words: "It was just relationship building. I said, yeah, I can do it."


═══ CLIP 2 — CHELSEA MEGGITT ═══

Now you might be thinking — okay, that's networking, but the real game is still the proposal you turn in. Chelsea Meggitt has been doing this since 2008. And she'll tell you the requirement itself gets shaped long before the RFP ever hits the street.

[CLIP: Chelsea Meggitt — "Once that RFP drops, no one's talking to anybody… you can be in the customer's ear and in the office all the time, shaping that requirement to fit ultimately what is best for you and for the government." @12:18 · Ep 266]

Hear that. By the time the solicitation posts, the conversation is over. The winners were in the room months earlier, helping write the very thing you're now scrambling to respond to.


═══ CLIP 3 — CHRIS MOORE ═══

And if you're sitting there thinking, "but I don't have the certifications, I don't have the past performance" — listen to Chris Moore. He went from zero contracts to three government deals in a single week. No big certs. He just showed up. Three meetings. Emails. Events. Over and over, until they knew his name.

[CLIP: Chris Moore — "It's when they need you, then that's when it's game… they can't trust you if they've never met you." @08:23 · Ep 253]

They can't trust you if they've never met you. He didn't out-write anybody. He out-showed-up everybody.

Three different people. Different industries, different states, never met each other. Same lesson.
