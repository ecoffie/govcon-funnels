═══ COLD OPEN ═══

The rule everyone was panicking about — CMMC — just got paused. And before you celebrate, there's a trap hiding inside that pause almost every small business is about to miss. Here's what it actually means.


═══ PAUSED ≠ CANCELLED ═══

Let's get the words right first, because the words are where people are already getting this wrong.

CMMC — that's the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification — did not get cancelled. It did not get watered down. It got paused. The implementation that was set to kick in this November got delayed while they reassess it through a 60-day RFI — a request for information. That's the government asking industry, "tell us what's working and what isn't," before they turn it back on.

So read my lips: pause is not the same as cancel. A pause has an end date. The clock didn't stop — somebody just hit the snooze button. And if you treat a snooze button like the alarm got thrown out the window, you're going to get caught flat-footed when it goes off again.

Everybody's celebrating like they dodged something. What actually happened is you got handed extra time. The question is what you do with it.


═══ THE $250K MYTH — AUDIT vs COMPLIANCE ═══

Now let's kill the number that's been scaring everybody. You've heard it — "CMMC is going to cost me two hundred and fifty thousand dollars." That number is doing a magic trick on you, and I want you to see how the trick works.

That two hundred fifty thousand dollars conflates two completely different things.

Thing one is the audit. The actual assessment — somebody coming in to verify you. That's about twenty thousand dollars. Not nothing, but not the monster you were sold.

Thing two is the cost of compliance — and that's where the big number comes from. That's the cost of actually implementing the 110 controls in NIST 800-171 — the endpoint management, the multi-factor authentication, all of it. That's the two hundred fifty.

Here's the part nobody says out loud. That compliance cost was never a surprise. Industry has been able to see this coming since 2015. Twenty fifteen. That's ten years of runway. So when somebody tells you the cost is the problem — the cost was never hiding. It was published. The audit isn't what's expensive. The work is what's expensive. And the work was always the assignment.

So when you hear "CMMC is too expensive," what you're usually hearing is "I didn't start when I was supposed to."


═══ WHO THIS HELPS vs WHO IT HURTS — THE 75% ═══

Here's the stat that reframes this whole conversation. Roughly seventy-five percent of small businesses in the defense supply chain have already invested and are already compliant. Three out of four. They did the work.

So who's actually complaining about this rule? Largely the ones who haven't done the work. And I say that with love, because I want you on the right side of this line.

Think about what the pause actually does. If you're in that seventy-five percent — you already did it — the pause is pure upside. You're compliant, your competitor isn't, and now you've got a window where that gap only gets wider. If you're in the group that stalled, the pause feels like a rescue, but it's not. It's a stay of execution. Same rule's coming back. The requirement didn't leave.

This is the trap I told you about at the top. The pause doesn't reward the people who fell behind. It rewards the people who were ready — by giving the behind crowd one more chance to stay behind while they celebrate.


═══ WHAT TO DO IN THE 60-DAY WINDOW ═══

So what do you actually do with this window? Let me give it to you straight.

First — understand how you're being asked to prove this today. Under the DFARS, right now, you self-attest. You check a box that says you're implementing NIST 171, and you submit an SPRS score. You're grading your own homework.

And here's why that matters more than ever during a pause. Those self-assessments have been found unreliable — people check the box without doing the work. But if you claim compliance you don't actually have, and then you get breached, you are exposed under the False Claims Act. That's not a paperwork slap. That's the government coming after you for lying to get paid. The pause does not make that risk go away. If anything, self-attesting to a score you can't back up is the most dangerous thing you can do right now.

Second — know your levels so you know your target. CMMC Level 1 is about seventeen basic requirements — that's the FAR-level, the foundational stuff. Level 2 is the full NIST 800-171. And the architect of this whole framework, Katie Arrington, the former DoD CISO and CIO — she'll walk you through exactly why it's built this way in the longer interview we're dropping alongside this video. Go watch that one next.

Third — use the sixty days the way the winners are using it. Not to relax. To close the gap. Get your real SPRS score honest. Start knocking out the 110 controls if you haven't. Because when the pause ends — and it will end — you don't want to be scrambling. You want to be done.


═══ CTA — CLOSE ═══

So here's the belief shift I want you to walk away with. Everybody thinks CMMC just got easier. It didn't. It got paused — and the real cost was never the audit, it was the work you were always supposed to do. This pause isn't a pardon. It's a head start. The only question is whether you use it.

And look — before you spend a dollar on compliance, make sure you even need it. Not every contract requires CMMC. So the smartest first move is to go find the contracts that actually do — the ones where being compliant makes you the obvious choice instead of one of the crowd.

That's exactly what Mindy does. Go to getmindy.ai and find the contracts that actually require CMMC — so your compliance work points at real money, not a maybe.
