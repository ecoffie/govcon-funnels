═══ COLD OPEN ═══

If you're a veteran, there is an entire system of government programs built to help you start and grow a business — money, training, mentorship, and a customer that specifically wants to buy from you.

And most veterans have no idea any of it exists.

I'm not talking about one loan you pay back with interest. I'm talking about a stack of programs — I'm going to walk you through all of them, one by one.

Last year the Department of Veterans Affairs alone spent almost sixty-seven billion dollars on contracts. And across the whole government, contracts set aside just for veteran-owned businesses came to about thirty-three billion dollars.

If you served, this world was practically built with you in mind. Let me show you exactly how it works — and what's changed, because a lot of the advice online is out of date.


═══ THE ADVANTAGE ═══

Here's the core thing to understand.

The government sets aside a portion of its contracts specifically for veteran-owned and service-disabled veteran-owned businesses.

That means when you compete for that work, you're competing against other veteran-owned businesses. Not the giant corporations. A much smaller field.

And here's a number that just changed. The government's goal for how much it spends with service-disabled veteran-owned businesses used to be three percent. Congress raised it to five percent starting in fiscal year twenty twenty-four.

And in that first year, the government didn't just hit it — it went over. Five point one four percent. About thirty-two point eight billion dollars, to service-disabled veteran-owned businesses.

So the demand isn't charity. It's a target the government is legally trying to hit, and it needs veteran-owned businesses to hit it.


═══ THE CERTIFICATION (WHAT CHANGED) ═══

Now, to unlock that advantage, you get certified as a veteran-owned business.

And I want to be precise here, because this changed recently and a lot of old information is floating around.

This certification used to be handled by the VA, through something called the CVE. As of January first, twenty twenty-three, it moved to the Small Business Administration — the SBA.

The program is now called VetCert. You apply through the SBA, at veterans-dot-certify-dot-sba-dot-gov.

So if you look this up and find old guides pointing you to the VA's old system, ignore them. You go through the SBA now. Get that one thing right and you save yourself months of confusion.

One more distinction. If you're a service-disabled veteran, that certification lets you compete for set-aside contracts across the entire government. If you're a veteran without a service-connected disability, the certification mainly helps you at the VA itself, under a program called Veterans First. Either way — you get certified through the SBA.


═══ THE PROGRAM STACK ═══

Okay. The advantage is the contracts. But there's a whole stack of programs to help you actually build the business that wins them. Let me go through them.

Number one. If you have a service-connected disability, look at Veteran Readiness and Employment — Chapter thirty-one. It used to be called Vocational Rehabilitation. It has a self-employment track, built specifically for veterans who want to start their own business.

Now I want to be careful and accurate about what that provides. It's help developing your business plan. Analysis of your business concept. Training in how to run a small business — the marketing, the finances. And guidance getting the resources to launch. It's real, hands-on support to get a business off the ground, and it exists for veterans with a disability rating.

Number two. Boots to Business. This is a free entrepreneurship course run by the SBA, part of the military transition program. Two days, introduction to starting a business. And if you're already out — there's a version called Boots to Business Reboot that's open to veterans of any era, in your community. Free.

Number three. Veterans Business Outreach Centers. There are more than thirty of them across the country, funded by the SBA. They give you one-on-one counseling, help with your business plan, mentorship, workshops — a real person to sit down with. Find the one near you.

Number four. The money side. The SBA runs an Express loan program — up to five hundred thousand dollars — and for veteran-owned businesses, the upfront guaranty fee on those loans is zero. That's a real, current benefit for this fiscal year. There's also a special disaster loan, the Military Reservist loan, if you're a reservist called to active duty and it hurts your business.

I'll put links to every one of these in the description so you don't have to write them down.


═══ WHAT YOU ALREADY HAVE ═══

And here's what I really want you to hear.

The skills you built in the military — discipline, following a process, getting things done, operating under pressure — those are exactly the skills this work rewards.

Government contracting is about doing what you say, on time, by the standard. That's a language you already speak.

You're not underqualified for this. In a lot of ways, you're overqualified.


═══ THE TURN — HOW YOU'D FIND THE WORK ═══

So the question becomes — what is the government actually buying, in what you do?

All of that spending is public. But it's spread across government websites that are a pain to search.

So I built a free tool that pulls it into one place. You type in what your business does, and it shows you which agencies are buying it and how much.

It's free. The link's in the description. Go see what's out there for a veteran-owned business like yours.


═══ CLOSE ═══

You already did the hard part. You served.

Now there's a customer that spends tens of billions of dollars a year and specifically wants to work with people like you — and a whole support system built to help you get there. The certification. The training. The mentorship. The money.

Most veterans never find out any of it exists. You just did.

Go see what they're buying — the free tool and the free starter guide are in the description.

Subscribe, and share this with a veteran who needs to hear it. I'll see you in the next one.

Program tour — every program named, verified current (Jul 2026)

Each is confirmed against a primary .gov source. The amber flags are the exact phrasings to AVOID on camera — the failure modes that would make you look dated or overclaim.

ADVANTAGE · government-wide

SDVOSB / VOSB set-asides

Contracts you compete for against only other vets. Government-wide goal raised to 5% (FY24 NDAA §863); FY24 actual 5.14% / $32.8B. Do NOT say "3%" — it's 5% since FY2024.

SBA · certification

VetCert

The certification that unlocks the set-asides. Moved VA→SBA, Jan 1 2023. Apply at veterans.certify.sba.gov. SDVOSB = government-wide; VOSB = VA-only (Vets First, PL 109-461, still in force). Do NOT say cert is done "through the VA."

VA · start the business

VR&E — Chapter 31

Veteran Readiness & Employment (renamed 2020 from Voc Rehab). Self-employment track: business-plan help, concept analysis, small-business training, launch guidance. Needs a service-connected disability rating (≥10–20%). Do NOT say it "buys equipment / gives startup capital" — unverified on the public page.

SBA / TAP · free training

Boots to Business

Free 2-day intro-to-entrepreneurship course, part of military transition (TAP). B2B Reboot opens it to veterans of any era + spouses. Delivered via SBA + VBOCs.

SBA · mentorship

VBOCs

Veterans Business Outreach Centers — more than 30 nationwide. One-on-one counseling, business-plan help, mentoring, workshops. The "real person to sit with."

SBA · the money

SBA Express $0 fee + MREIDL

$0 upfront guaranty fee on SBA Express loans (≤$500K) to veteran-owned businesses — verified for FY2026 (Info Notice 5000-872051). Plus MREIDL (Military Reservist disaster loan, up to $2M @ 4%). Do NOT say "fee waiver on ALL SBA loans" — only Express is verified.

✂
Cut for accuracy (say them only if re-verified at film time): the old broad "SBA Veterans Advantage" fee waiver on regular 7(a) loans appears gone — don't cite it. VIP (Veteran Institute for Procurement) is real but a nonprofit, SBA-funded — don't call it a "government program." State-level programs are out of scope (federal only).
