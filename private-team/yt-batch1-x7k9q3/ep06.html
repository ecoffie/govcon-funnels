═══ COLD OPEN ═══

For two years everyone's been arguing about the political drama — who got cut, who got funded. Almost nobody's talking about the part that actually matters if you sell to the government: it quietly changed HOW it buys. A faster lane opened up, and billions of dollars moved into it.

Not just what it buys — HOW it buys. And one part of the military just built a literal front door for small businesses that have never done this before.

I'm going to show you three shifts, with the real numbers. No politics. Just where the money moved, and how you get in front of it.


═══ THE ONE HONEST RULE ═══

Quick thing first, so we stay honest — because the internet isn't.

An executive order doesn't spend a dollar. Only Congress can do that. What these orders do is DIRECT the government — they redirect HOW it buys and who it prioritizes. That's the whole trick to reading this stuff: don't watch what got announced, watch where the money actually moved.

And it moved. Hundreds of billions a year run through these buying rules. When the rules change, the opportunities change. See that early, and you're ahead of almost everyone.

Three shifts. Here's the first, and it's the big one.


═══ SHIFT 1 — THE FASTER LANE (OTAs) ═══

For decades, selling to the government meant one painful path: the Federal Acquisition Regulation. The FAR. Two thousand pages of rules, clauses, and audits. It's the reason people say government contracting is slow, and that only the big guys can play.

But there's another path — and the government is pouring money into it. It's called an Other Transaction. An OT.

In plain English: it's a way for the government to buy research, prototypes, and new technology OUTSIDE most of those FAR rules. Faster. More flexible. Built specifically to work with commercial companies and startups that would never touch a traditional government contract.

Here's the number that tells the whole story. Follow the money on this one. In twenty sixteen, the Defense Department spent about one point eight billion dollars this way. By twenty twenty-four, it was over eighteen billion. That's roughly ten times more, in eight years.

Most of that — around sixteen billion — is prototype work. New tech. Which means if you build something, this is the lane where the government is most willing to try you without a twenty-year track record.

And here's the part that should really get your attention. If you win one of these prototype deals — one that was competed fairly — the government can turn around and award you the full production contract without making you compete all over again. Win the prototype, and the big contract can follow, directly.

Now here's what nobody tells beginners. You don't chase these one at a time. A lot of this money flows through groups called consortiums — organized around missions like space, medical, mobility, cyber. About half of all this prototype money runs through them. You join the consortium, and you get visibility into the work.

And joining is cheap. A lot of them run somewhere between free and about fifteen hundred dollars a year. That's the actual on-ramp. Not a secret. Just quiet.


═══ SHIFT 2 — THEY BUILT A FRONT DOOR (LYNX) ═══

Second shift. And this one's brand new — it launched at the start of twenty twenty-six.

The Department of Defense — which this administration now brands the "Department of War," and I want to be clear, that's a branding title, the legal name is still the Department of Defense — through its Office of Small Business Programs, built a free platform called Lynx.

Now let me be precise, because this is exactly where people online are already getting it wrong.

Lynx is NOT a store where you buy contracts. It's not a marketplace. It won't hand you an award.

What it is: a front door. You go to lynxconnect-dot-io, you build a company profile, you take a readiness assessment, and it gives you a roadmap — where you stand, what gaps to close — and it matches you to partners and opportunities in the defense world. Readiness and matchmaking. That's the tool.

Think of it as the government finally admitting the on-ramp was too confusing, and building a guide for exactly the small, non-traditional business that has good technology but no idea how to navigate the Pentagon.

It's free. It won't win the contract for you. But if you've ever thought, "I make something the military could use, but I don't know where to start" — that's the door they built for you.


═══ SHIFT 3 — THE SBA GOT REBUILT ═══

Third shift. The Small Business Administration — the agency behind a lot of your certifications and loans — got completely restructured. Twice. Once in early twenty twenty-five, and again in twenty twenty-six.

And this one's a genuine mixed bag, so I'll give it to you straight.

The good, if you're trying to get certified: it got faster and simpler. There's now one unified system where you can apply for up to four certifications at once. The veteran certification backlog that used to take months got cleared out. And some requirements — like the HUBZone residency rule — got loosened.

The catch: the agency itself got a lot smaller. Its workforce was cut by more than half. Its budget dropped by about a third. Offices got consolidated. Some of the local counseling and outreach programs went away.

So here's the honest translation: the paperwork to get certified got faster — but the number of humans standing by to hold your hand through it got smaller. More self-service, less local help. Know that going in.


═══ THE MAP — WHERE IT'S ALL FLOWING ═══

Step back and look at the whole board.

The biggest buckets are still the biggest. Defense — over four hundred billion dollars a year. Veterans Affairs, around sixty-seven billion. Energy, in the forties. Health, around thirty.

These three shifts don't change those buckets. They change how you get into them. A faster lane you can actually use. A front door if you're new. And a certification system that got quicker, even as the help got thinner.

If you can see that, you can position on it. That's the difference between watching the drama and following the money.


═══ THE TURN — HOW YOU'D WATCH IT YOURSELF ═══

So — how do you actually see where this money's moving, in your own industry, right now?

Every one of these dollars is public. Every contract, every agency, every trend. The problem is it's scattered across government websites that are miserable to use.

So I built a free tool that pulls it into one place. You type in what you do, and it shows you which agencies are spending on it, and how that's trending. That's how you watch the money instead of guessing.

SHIFT 1

The faster lane — OTAs (Other Transactions)

Verified

The money is moving here: DoD OTA obligations $1.8B (FY16) → $18B+ (FY24) — ~10x in 8 yrs (FY24: ~$16B prototype + ~$2B production). FY21–24 = $62.9B total, 90% prototype; consortia = ~half of prototype $. Buys R&D/prototypes OUTSIDE the FAR. The carrot: win a competed prototype OTA → production can be awarded with NO re-compete (10 U.S.C. §4022(f)). On-ramp: consortia (S2MARTS, SpEC, NAMC, MTEC), membership often $0–$1,500/yr.

Source: GAO-25-107546 (Sep 2025) + DoD OTA Overview Guide (17 Dec 2025). Statutes: research §4021 / prototype §4022 (NOT the old "2371b" — renumbered 2021). ⚠ No % of OTA $ to small biz (GAO says DoD doesn't track it). ATI/NSTXL totals are pass-through to members, not kept.

The front door — Lynx (lynxconnect.io)

Reported ×4

⚠ #1 LANDMINE: Lynx is NOT a marketplace / contract vehicle / OTA on-ramp / place you win contracts. It's readiness + matchmaking. Don't attach $ or user counts (none published). SHIFT 3

The SBA overhaul — faster certs, leaner agency

Verified

The win: unified MySBA portal (4 certs at once), VetCert backlog cleared (~81→~12 days), HUBZone residency 6mo→90 days. The catch: workforce −50%+, budget −33%, offices consolidated to HQ, some counseling/outreach cut. Admin: Kelly Loeffler. Two reorgs: Mar 2025 + Jun 2026.

⚠ Don't conflate the two reorgs (43% Mar '25 plan vs "over 50%" cumulative Jun '26). HUBZone/WOSB specifics are REPORTED (not confirmed rule text). Don't say "SBA now runs student loans" — only announced. VetCert ~12 days is REPORTED.

🚫 Do not say

The Lynx one corrects your own working assumption

Say any of these and a knowledgeable commenter torches your credibility. The Lynx one is the big one.

✕ "Lynx is a marketplace / contract vehicle / where you win contracts." → It's a READINESS + MATCHMAKING tool. It won't award you anything. Say "a free front door to get ready + get matched."
✕ "The Department of War" is the new name. → It's a branding title (EO 14347, Sept 2025). Legal name is still Department of Defense. Say "which the administration now brands the Department of War."
✕ Any % / count of OTA dollars going to small business. → Not tracked (that's GAO's whole complaint). State the $1.8B→$18B growth; don't invent a small-biz share.
✕ "OTAs have no rules / no oversight." → They're outside the FAR, not outside all law. Say "outside most FAR rules."
✕ Conflate prototype ($16B) with total ($18B) OTA spend. → Keep them distinct if you cite both.
✕ "SBA now services student loans." → Only ANNOUNCED (Mar 2025), never confirmed executed. Say "Trump announced he wanted SBA to take it over."
✕ "43% cut" = "over 50% cut." → 43% was the Mar 2025 plan; "over 50%" is the cumulative figure in the Jun 2026 release. Don't merge them.
✕ "An executive order funded X." → Orders DIRECT / RESHAPE. Only Congress funds.
