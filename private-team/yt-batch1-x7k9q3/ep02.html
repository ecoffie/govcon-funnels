═══ COLD OPEN ═══

If I had to start completely over — no experience, no connections, no track record — and win a hundred-thousand-dollar government contract this year…

here's exactly what I'd do.

Step by step. In order. The way I'd actually do it.

And I want to be honest with you up front — this isn't a get-rich-quick thing. It's a real path. But it's a known path. People do this every single day.

So if you're starting from zero, this is your roadmap. Let's go.


═══ STEP ONE — GET IN THE SYSTEM ═══

Step one. Get registered. For free.

Before the government can pay you a dollar, you have to exist in their system.

That means getting something called a UEI — a unique ID number for your business — and registering on a site called SAM.gov.

Both are free. Let me say that louder — both are completely free.

There are companies that will try to charge you hundreds of dollars to do this for you. Don't. It's a scam. You do it yourself, for nothing.

That's step one. You're now in the system.


═══ STEP TWO — PICK WHAT YOU SELL ═══

Step two. Decide what you're actually selling.

The government organizes everything it buys by codes. Cleaning has a code. Consulting has a code. Landscaping, catering, IT — every service has one.

You pick the codes that match what you can actually do. Not what sounds impressive. What you can deliver.

Keep it honest and keep it simple. This is how the government will find you, and how you'll find the work.


═══ STEP THREE — FIND ONE REAL OPPORTUNITY ═══

Step three. Find one opportunity. Just one.

This is where most beginners mess up. They try to bid on everything and win nothing.

Instead, find a single contract that fits what you do — something small, something realistic for a first win.

The government posts these publicly. And the smart move is to look for the smaller, less glamorous ones. Everybody's chasing the big flashy contracts. The hundred-thousand-dollar cleaning or service contract? Far less competition.


═══ STEP FOUR — DO YOUR HOMEWORK ═══

Step four. Study it like it's the only one.

Who's the agency? What have they bought before? Who has the contract now — because most contracts get re-competed, and knowing who holds it tells you who you're up against.

Read the whole thing. The requirements. The deadline. What they're actually evaluating.

This is called capture. One contract you understand deeply beats ten you blindly applied to.


═══ STEP FIVE — TALK TO A HUMAN ═══

Step five. Reach out before the bid.

Every agency has people whose entire job is helping small businesses like you. They're called small-business specialists. Their contact info is public.

A short, respectful email introducing yourself is completely normal. This is what they're there for.

Do it before the contract posts — because once it's live, they often can't talk to you. Early is everything.


═══ STEP SIX — SUBMIT THE BID ═══

Step six. Actually submit the bid. This is the one everybody skips — and it's the one everybody's scared of.

Here's the truth. Two things stop beginners cold. "I don't know how to write a proposal." And "I have no idea what to charge."

So let me show you how I handle both — because the tool I built has features for exactly this. Let me pull it up.

First, the writing. Inside Mindy there's a Proposal Assist feature. You give it the solicitation, and it drafts your response for you — the technical section, the management section, past performance, all of it. It even builds what's called a compliance matrix — every single "shall" and "must" the government buried in the document, pulled out into a checklist so you don't miss one.

Now — I want to be straight with you. It gives you a strong starting draft, not a finished submission. You still review it, you add your own proof, you make it yours. But going from a blank page to a real draft in minutes? That's the difference between bidding and never bidding.

Then there's the pricing. This is the other thing that freezes people. So there's a Pricing Intel feature — you put in your industry, and it pulls the real hourly rates the government has actually paid, from real awarded contracts. And it gives you three targets — aggressive if you want to win on price, competitive which is the safe market rate, and premium. So you're not guessing what to charge. You're pricing off real data.

I'll be honest — those two features are on the paid plan. But the free account is where you start, and it's where everything up to this point lives.

Then you follow their instructions exactly — miss one required form and you're thrown out before they even read it — and you submit. On time. And you're officially in the game.

Most people never get here. They research forever and never actually bid. Don't be that person. The only bid that can win is the one you turn in.


═══ THE TURN — HOW YOU'D FIND THE CONTRACT ═══

Now — steps three and four is where people get stuck. "How do I actually find the right opportunity? Where do I even look?"

All of this is public. The problem is it lives on clunky government websites nobody wants to dig through.

So I built a free tool that pulls it into one place. You type in what you do, and it shows you what the government is buying and who's buying it.

It's free — no credit card. The link's in the description. Go make a free account and type in your service right now — that's literally step three, done in two minutes.

No experience required. Just the willingness to follow the steps most people never bother to learn.

Your first contract might take a few tries. That's normal. But this is how it's done.
