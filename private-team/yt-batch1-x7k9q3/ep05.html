═══ COLD OPEN ═══

The most profitable businesses in America right now aren't the exciting ones.

They're boring.

Cleaning. Landscaping. Staffing. Cutting the grass.

And a lot of the people running them have found one customer that pays on time, every time, and never runs out of money.

The United States government.

I pulled the real numbers — what the government actually spent, last year, on nine businesses so ordinary you'd walk right past them.

Some of these will genuinely surprise you.

And the last one? The last one is over thirty-two billion dollars.

Let's count them down.


═══ NUMBER NINE — LANDSCAPING ═══

Number nine. Landscaping.

Cutting grass. Trimming hedges. Maintaining grounds.

Last year the federal government spent over half a billion dollars — about five hundred and twenty million — on landscaping.

Not a tech company. Not a defense giant. Lawn care.

Somewhere out there is a landscaping crew with a government contract, mowing the same kind of grass their competitor mows — except they're getting paid by the most reliable customer on earth.


═══ NUMBER EIGHT — WAREHOUSING ═══

Number eight. Warehousing and storage.

Just holding stuff. Space, shelves, a forklift.

Over seven hundred million dollars last year.

If you've got a warehouse, the government needs somewhere to put things. That's the entire business.


═══ NUMBER SEVEN — EQUIPMENT REPAIR ═══

Number seven. Equipment repair and maintenance.

Fixing things that break. Machines, systems, gear.

Almost seven hundred million dollars.

Every piece of equipment the government owns eventually breaks. Somebody gets paid to fix it. Why not you?


═══ NUMBER SIX — TEMP STAFFING ═══

Number six. Temporary staffing.

Providing people. Filling shifts.

Nearly a billion dollars — nine hundred and forty-six million.

You don't even need to do the work yourself. You supply the workers. The government has been paying staffing agencies to do exactly that for decades.


═══ NUMBER FIVE — HVAC & PLUMBING ═══

Number five. Heating, plumbing, and air conditioning.

The trades. HVAC. Pipes.

Almost one point one billion dollars last year.

Every federal building has heating that breaks and pipes that leak. That's not a tech problem. That's a guy with a van and a license.


═══ NUMBER FOUR — FOOD SERVICE ═══

Number four. Food service.

Feeding people. Running cafeterias, catering, meals.

One point three billion dollars.

The government feeds a lot of people. Somebody's got that contract. It could be a catering business that looks exactly like one in your town.


═══ NUMBER THREE — JANITORIAL ═══

Number three. Janitorial services.

Cleaning. Mopping floors. Taking out the trash.

Over one and a half billion dollars.

Let me say that again. The government spent more than one and a half billion dollars last year on cleaning.

The most unglamorous business you can think of — cleaning buildings — is a billion-and-a-half-dollar-a-year market with the government alone.


═══ NUMBER TWO — SECURITY GUARDS ═══

Number two. Security guards.

Standing watch. Guarding buildings and gates.

Over four and a half billion dollars.

Not high-tech surveillance. People. Guards. The government pays more than four and a half billion dollars a year for someone to stand at the door.


═══ NUMBER ONE — FACILITIES SUPPORT ═══

And number one.

Facilities support.

Basically — running and maintaining buildings. Keeping the lights on, the place operating, the whole thing working.

Thirty-two point three billion dollars.

Last year. On facilities.

That is not a rounding error. That's bigger than the entire revenue of most companies you've heard of — spent on the least exciting work imaginable.

Running buildings.


═══ THE TURN — HOW YOU'D FIND YOURS ═══

Now here's the point.

Every single one of these is a normal business. Nothing special. Nothing high-tech.

The difference between the owner getting a government check and the owner who isn't — is that one of them looked, and the other never did.

So the question is: is what YOU do on a list like this?

Because it probably is.

All of this spending is public — but knowing where to look and what to do first is the hard part.

So I put the whole starting playbook into a free guide. How to find what the government's buying in your industry, how to get in the system, and the exact first steps — no fluff, no cost.

It's completely free. The link's in the description. Grab it, and you'll have the real roadmap in front of you tonight.


═══ CLOSE ═══

The boring businesses are winning.

Not because they're special. Because they found the customer nobody else is looking at.

Thirty-two billion on facilities. Four and a half billion on guards. A billion and a half on cleaning.

Ordinary work. Extraordinary customer.

Grab the free guide in the description — it's the roadmap to getting your first contract.

And subscribe, because I'm breaking down this whole game, one video at a time.

I'll see you in the next one.
