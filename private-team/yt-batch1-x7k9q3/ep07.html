═══ COLD OPEN ═══

There's a county in Indiana with about ten thousand people in it.

Fewer people than a single high school football stadium holds.

And last year, the federal government spent roughly eight hundred million dollars there.

Eight hundred million. In a place most Americans have never heard of.

That's not a fluke. All over the country there are tiny places quietly getting rich off federal spending…

while everyone assumes all the money is in Washington.

It isn't. Let me show you five of them. The last one will surprise you.


═══ THE PATTERN ═══

Here's the pattern nobody talks about.

Federal money doesn't follow the politicians. It follows the work.

A base. A shipyard. A research lab. A depot.

Drop one of those into a small town, and that little community suddenly starts pulling in numbers that don't make sense for its size.

And the money doesn't stop at the fence line. It spills into the town around it — the machine shop, the IT company, the staffing firm, the caterer, the construction crew.

None of them are famous. All of them are eating.

These are real places. Real numbers. All public data. Let's go.


═══ NUMBER FIVE — THE INDIANA ONE ═══

Number five. That little county in Indiana I opened with.

Around ten thousand people. Roughly seven hundred and sixty million federal dollars a year.

Why? There's a Navy facility sitting right there — one of the biggest of its kind.

And around it, a whole ecosystem of contractors. The kind of businesses you'd never guess were billing the government.


═══ NUMBER FOUR — THE KANSAS ONE ═══

Number four. A county in Kansas.

Around half a billion federal dollars a year. In a place people drive past without a second thought.

An Army installation anchors it. And a base doesn't just employ soldiers — it buys. Food. Maintenance. Construction. Services.

If you run a business anywhere near one, the government might be the biggest customer you've never called.


═══ NUMBER THREE — THE NEBRASKA ONE ═══

Number three. A county in Nebraska.

Around half a billion dollars a year in federal spending.

Nebraska. Not exactly where you picture defense money going.

But there's a major military command headquartered there. And the businesses that support it — the contractors, the suppliers, the specialists — are quietly building real wealth in the middle of the country, where nobody's watching.


═══ NUMBER TWO — THE WASHINGTON ONE ═══

Number two. A county in Washington State.

Over a billion dollars a year. More than one and a quarter billion.

This one's a shipyard community. And shipyards are contractor magnets — welding, electrical, engineering, logistics, security.

An entire local economy built on one fact: the Navy needs its ships maintained. Most of those companies started small, local, and completely unknown.


═══ NUMBER ONE — THE BIG ONE ═══

And number one — the big one.

There's a county in Alabama that pulled in over eleven billion federal dollars last year.

Eleven billion. In Alabama.

It's built around a place people nicknamed "Rocket City" — decades of space and defense work turned a mid-size Southern town into one of the biggest federal-contracting hubs in the entire country.

And here's the part that matters for you: the giants get the headlines, but thousands of small businesses feed that ecosystem. The spending is so enormous that the crumbs are a fortune.


═══ THE TURN — HOW YOU'D FIND IT ═══

So here's the lesson in all five.

The money isn't where you think. It's where the work is. And the work is often in places nobody's paying attention to.

Which means the real question isn't "is there government money?" It's — "is there government money near ME, and how would I even go after it?"

So I put the whole starting playbook into a free guide. How this works, how to find what the government's buying near you, and the exact first steps to go from watching this video to actually being in the game.

It's completely free. The link's in the description. Grab it — you might be sitting next to a goldmine and have no idea.
