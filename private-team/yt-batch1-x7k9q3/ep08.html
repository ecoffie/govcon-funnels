═══ COLD OPEN ═══

Last year the United States government spent more than fourteen billion dollars on one thing.

Consultants.

People who give advice. Slideshows. Recommendations.

Fourteen billion dollars.

And when I pulled the list of who actually gets that money — it was not what most people expect.

Let me show you exactly where fourteen billion dollars in "advice" goes. The real names. The real numbers.

Because once you see how this works, you'll understand a game that's been running in plain sight for decades.


═══ THE NUMBER ═══

First, let's be clear about the number.

Over fourteen billion dollars. In one year. On management consulting.

That's just one category. Advice on how to run things.

Not building anything. Not manufacturing anything. Consulting.

If you've ever wondered where a chunk of your tax dollars goes — some of it goes to people making recommendations in conference rooms.


═══ WHO GETS IT ═══

Now — who actually gets this money?

The single biggest consulting recipient last year was Deloitte. Over one billion dollars. From one firm.

Right behind them, a company called Chemonics. Also over a billion.

Then names you might know — Booz Allen Hamilton. Guidehouse. Even BCG, the Boston Consulting Group, has a federal arm.

These firms have built entire businesses on one customer: the government.

Billions of dollars a year, to tell agencies how to do their jobs.


═══ THE PART NOBODY SAYS ═══

Here's the part nobody says out loud.

You might look at that and think it's crazy. Fourteen billion on advice.

But here's the flip side — and this is the part that matters for you.

If the government is willing to pay Deloitte a billion dollars for expertise…

they are also buying expertise from much smaller companies. Specialists. Niche experts. One-person firms with deep knowledge in a narrow area.

The big names take the headlines. But the government buys advice at every level — and a lot of it goes to small businesses you've never heard of.


═══ WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU ═══

So think about what you actually know.

Are you an expert in something? A process, an industry, a technical skill, a compliance area?

Because "consulting" isn't one thing. It's thousands of narrow specialties.

The government needs people who understand cybersecurity. Logistics. Healthcare systems. Training. Data. Project management.

If you're genuinely good at something — there's a real chance an agency somewhere is paying for exactly that expertise right now.

You don't have to be Deloitte. You have to be the right specialist.


═══ THE TURN — HOW YOU'D FIND IT ═══

And here's the question that stops most people.

"Okay — but how would I even see which agencies are buying my kind of expertise?"

Fair. Because all of this is public — every consulting contract, every firm, every dollar — but knowing where to look and what to do first is the hard part.

So I put the whole starting playbook into a free guide. How this world works, how to find which agencies buy your kind of expertise, and the exact first steps — no fluff, no cost.

It's completely free. The link's in the description. Grab it, and you'll have the real roadmap in front of you tonight.


═══ CLOSE ═══

Fourteen billion dollars a year. On advice.

The big firms figured this out a long time ago. That's why they're huge.

But the door isn't only open to them. The government buys expertise at every size — you just have to know it's there, and know where to look.

Grab the free guide in the description — it's the roadmap to landing your first federal contract.

And subscribe, because I break down exactly how this world works, one video at a time.

I'll see you in the next one.
