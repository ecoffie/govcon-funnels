═══ COLD OPEN ═══

There's a lot of money in government contracting — and wherever there's money, there are people trying to take a cut of yours before you earn a dime. I've watched good, honest business owners get taken. Here are the scams — starting with the one that charges you for something completely free.

Not because those business owners were foolish. Because nobody warned them.

So this is your warning. Save it. Send it to anyone you know who's just getting started — because this is the video that keeps them from getting taken.


═══ SCAM #1 — PAYING TO REGISTER ═══

Number one, and this is the big one — the one I need you to hear before anything else.

Anyone who tries to charge you to register in SAM.gov is running a scam.

SAM.gov registration is free. Completely free. You do it yourself, directly on the government's own site. Getting your business ID — your UEI — is free too. It's all free.

But there are companies — official-looking ones — that will call you, email you, even mail you letters, offering to "register you" for hundreds, sometimes thousands of dollars.

They're charging you for something the government does for nothing. That's the whole scam, right there. Never pay it. That is the one fee you should never pay.

If you remember nothing else from this video, remember that one. It's the most common way new contractors get taken.


═══ SCAM #2 — FAKE RENEWAL NOTICES ═══

Number two. Fake renewal notices.

Once you're registered, you'll start getting official-looking notices telling you your registration is expiring and you have to pay to renew.

Same trick. Renewing is free — on the government site, you do it yourself. These notices are designed to look scary and official so you panic and pay.

If it asks for a payment to renew your SAM registration, it is not the government. Delete it.


═══ SCAM #3 — GUARANTEED CONTRACTS ═══

Number three. Anyone guaranteeing you a contract.

Nobody can guarantee you'll win a government contract. Nobody.

If someone promises you guaranteed awards in exchange for a fee, they're lying to you. The government awards contracts through a competitive process. There is no secret backdoor you can buy your way through.

A guarantee is the single clearest sign you're talking to a scammer. The second you hear it, walk away.


═══ SCAM #4 — FAKE OPPORTUNITY EMAILS ═══

Number four. Fake opportunity emails.

You'll get emails claiming there's an urgent contract "perfect for you" — just click here, or pay a small fee to access it, or hand over your information.

Real opportunities are posted publicly and freely. If an "exclusive opportunity" needs a payment or a suspicious link before you can even see it, it's bait. Don't take it.


═══ SCAM #5 — OVERPRICED "CERTIFICATION HELP" ═══

Number five. Overpriced certification help.

Getting certified — as a small business, woman-owned, veteran-owned — is done through the government, and the certification itself doesn't cost a fee.

Now, let me be fair here — legitimate consultants do exist, and paying someone to help with the paperwork can absolutely be worth it. I'm not telling you all paid help is a scam. But be careful. Some charge outrageous prices for something you can often do yourself with a little patience. Know what it should cost before you hand anyone your money.


═══ SCAM #6 — THE "SECRET INSIDER LIST" ═══

Number six. The secret insider list.

Somebody selling you a "secret list of contracts nobody else knows about."

There is no secret list. The opportunities are public — that's the law. Anyone selling you secrecy is selling you something that's already free.


═══ SCAM #7 — UPFRONT "SETUP" FEES ═══

Number seven. Big upfront setup fees.

Be very cautious of anyone demanding a large payment upfront to "set up your whole government contracting business" — with vague promises about everything you'll supposedly get.

Real help is specific and transparent. Vague, expensive, and upfront is the pattern of a scam. Every time.


═══ THE TURN — THE FREE TOOLS ARE THE REAL ONES ═══

Here's the throughline running under all seven.

The real tools in this world are free. SAM.gov is free. The opportunity data is public. The certifications don't charge a fee. The scam is always the same move — someone charging you for access to something that was free the whole time.

Once you see that pattern, you can't be taken. That's the whole point of this video.

And honestly, that's why I built a free tool — to pull all that public data into one place so you don't have to pay anyone to see it. You type in what you do, and it shows you what the government's buying. No credit card, no fee. Just the real thing, out in the open.
